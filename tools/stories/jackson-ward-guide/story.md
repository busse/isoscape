# Seeing the change: a walking guide to Jackson Ward

## How to use this guide

The walk has eight stops and is {{km}} km ({{miles}} miles), about {{minutes}} minutes on foot at
about {{pace}} m a minute, not counting time at the stops. It starts at Second and Leigh Streets
and ends across East Leigh Street from the Coliseum, {{back_m}} m ({{back_ft}} feet) from where it
began. Every stop is on a public sidewalk.

::: figure
![The whole walk in Jackson Ward, north up: eight numbered stops along a green line from Second and Leigh Streets to the Coliseum, the I-95 trench in gray, Gilpin Court as a bordered area, and two dashed frames marking the west and east maps](jackson-ward-guide-map-phone.webp)

The whole walk, north up. The dashed frames are the west and east maps further down, which name
more streets. *How this was made:* drawn in code from OpenStreetMap; the 1952 street lines the
[story](jackson-ward.md#the-streets-the-highway-cut) read off the Sanborn sheets, with seven
stretches joined across the trench as the U.S. Geological Survey's map of 1933–34 shows them; and
City of Richmond parcel records for Gilpin Court's outline, which is approximate.
:::

Each stop starts with directions from the stop before it, then says where to stand and what you
are looking at. The route keeps to mapped sidewalks and crossings, and the directions name each
crossing with what is there: a signal, a marked crosswalk, or no marked crosswalk. The route and
directions follow the sidewalks and crossings OpenStreetMap showed on {{osm_date}}.

Seven stops have a picture of the same few blocks in 1952 and today, seen from the south as if
from a low airplane. The numbered disc in the picture is where you stand. On the maps, dashed
streets named in italic are streets of 1952 that the highway's trench cut.

Stops 1 to 4 and stop 8 are on the east map.

::: figure
![The east map, north up: stops 1 to 4 and 8, Second Street, the North 1st Street bridge, Bates Street and the Coliseum, with Cave, Paradise, Cottage and Gilbert Alleys among the cut streets dashed in italic; stop 5 is at its west edge](jackson-ward-guide-map-east.webp)

The east map: stops 1 to 4 and 8, with stop 5 at its west edge.
:::

## 1. Second Street at Leigh Street

{{directions_1}}

**Stand** {{where_1}}. **Look** {{sees_1}}.

{{pair_1}}

Second Street, "the Deuce," was the ward's main commercial street [[Community Plan
2026](#sources)]. It was known as Black Wall Street by 1903, the National Park Service says
[[NPS, Black Wall Street](#sources)].

The 1925 Sanborn sheet's Miller Hotel stood at the corner of 2nd and Leigh. Neverett A. Eggleston
Sr. leased it in 1939, bought it in 1942 and renamed it the Hotel Eggleston; it was demolished in
2009 after a partial collapse [[UVA, Green Book project](#sources)].

## 2. Where Second Street stops

{{directions_2}}

**Stand** {{where_2}}. **Look** {{sees_2}}.

{{pair_2}}

Seven streets that ran through at street level now end at the highway's trench or cross it on a
bridge [[the story](jackson-ward.md#the-streets-the-highway-cut)]. Today's map shows that 2nd
Street is one of those that end. The Richmond–Petersburg Turnpike Authority built its toll road
through the ward between 1955 and 1958 [[Acts of Assembly 1954](#sources); [Community Plan
2026](#sources), timeline]. The road now carries I-95 and, through the ward, I-64 as well
[[Feasibility Study 2022](#sources), §1.4.3].

Under the highway the U.S. Geological Survey's lidar shows the trench about 80–100 m wide and 3
to 6.5 m deep under the old cross streets [[the story](jackson-ward.md#the-ground-before-the-trench)].

## 3. North 1st Street bridge

{{directions_3}}

**Stand** {{where_3}}. **Look** {{sees_3}}.

{{pair_3}}

The 1952 Sanborn sheets show {{paved}} buildings inside today's paved roadway and ramps, and
{{corridor}} in the corridor of that pavement plus 15 m on each side, from the Belvidere Street
ramps to the line of 8th Street. These are outlines on a map; the number does not count homes,
families or people, and it is approximate [[the
story](jackson-ward.md#how-many-buildings-stood-in-the-highways-path)].

East of about 3rd Street, north of Leigh, the corridor runs through Navy Hill [[the
story](jackson-ward.md#how-many-buildings-stood-in-the-highways-path)]. Published counts of what
the road destroyed differ, and the story sets them side by side.

## 4. Bates Street and the JXN Haus

{{directions_4}}

**Stand** {{where_4}}. **Look** {{sees_4}}.

{{pair_4}}

In 1957 the Turnpike Authority and the city took by eminent domain a cottage built in 1793 by
Abraham Peyton Skipwith, who had bought his freedom. It was sold for about $25 and moved to
Goochland County [[LVA, House to Highway](#sources); [Axios, April 2026](#sources)].

The JXN Project, founded by the sisters Enjoli and Sesha Moon, rebuilt the cottage in Jackson Ward
as a reconstruction of the original. It opened on Bates Street in April 2026 with the JXN Haus,
the project's headquarters [[The Richmonder, April 2026](#sources); [Richmond Magazine, May
2026](#sources)].

Stops 5, 6 and 7 are on the west map. The walk to stop 5 starts on the east map and ends on this
one.

## 5. Sixth Mount Zion Baptist Church

{{directions_5}}

**Stand** {{where_5}}. **Look** {{sees_5}}.

{{pair_5}}

Sixth Mount Zion was organized by John Jasper in 1867 and was in the ward from 1869
[[Community Plan 2026](#sources); [Feasibility Study 2022](#sources), §1.4]. It stood at the edge
of the highway's path and was spared in 1956. The state's register nomination says it was allowed
to stand because of its historical significance, credits its pastor, Dr. Augustus Walter Brown
Sr., with saving it, and notes that the interstate swings around it [[DHR 127-0472](#sources)].

Wikipedia, citing Benjamin Campbell, says the road was rerouted slightly [[Campbell
2012](#sources), via Wikipedia].

## 6. Chamberlayne Parkway bridge

{{directions_6}}

**Stand** {{where_6}}. **Look** {{sees_6}}.

{{pair_6}}

The trench is up to about 8 m deep between Brook Road and Chamberlayne Parkway [[the
story](jackson-ward.md#the-ground-before-the-trench)].

A cap or lid over the highway is being studied. The 2022 feasibility study found it would be
"technically possible" to build a bridge or lid in a study area from the Belvidere Street bridge to the North
1st Street bridge, and found four of its six areas feasible, from Chamberlayne Parkway to North
1st Street [[Feasibility Study 2022](#sources), §3.2.4; [Fact sheet 2023](#sources)]. People in
and around the ward hold a range of views on the cap, as Elizabeth McGowan reported in July 2024
[[VCIJ 2024](#sources)]. This guide takes no position on it.

## 7. Gilpin Court, where Apostle Town stood

{{directions_7}}

**Stand** {{where_7}}. **Look** {{sees_7}}.

{{pair_7}}

On January 16, 1941, City Council passed the ordinance that began clearing Apostle Town, a section
of Jackson Ward [[LVA 2025](#sources)]. The Richmond Housing Authority built Gilpin Court there; it
was dedicated on April 28, 1943, with 301 units [[Feasibility Study 2022](#sources), §1.4.2;
[LVA 2025](#sources)]. Gilpin Court was enlarged in 1957 to house families the Turnpike displaced
[[Encyclopedia Virginia](#sources)].

People live in Gilpin Court today, and this guide describes it as homes. In July 2026 a draft
agreement between the city and the Richmond Redevelopment and Housing Authority (RRHA) called for
one-for-one replacement of all 781 units [[Gilpin Court MOU 2026](#sources)]. This guide takes no
position on the plan.

## 8. The Coliseum site

{{directions_8}} Stop 8 is back on the [east map](#how-to-use-this-guide).

**Stand** {{where_8}}. **Look** {{sees_8}}.

{{pair_8}}

Navy Hill had become a majority-Black neighborhood in the early twentieth century, Harry Kollatz
Jr. writes, and the Coliseum was later built on its cleared blocks [[Kollatz 2019](#sources)]. A
photograph shows it under construction on December 30, 1968 [[WVTF 2016](#sources)]. On November
19, 2026, it still stood, and the city was aiming to demolish it by the end of 2027 [[WWBT,
November 2026](#sources)].

## About this guide

Chris Busse, a Richmond artist, made this guide with Claude. Its history comes from the page
[Jackson Ward, Richmond, VA, before I-95, block by block](jackson-ward.md) and that page's
sources, which each statement cites. The route, the directions and the distances were laid out in
code from OpenStreetMap as it stood on {{osm_date}}.

The maps are drawn in code from OpenStreetMap, the Sanborn fire-insurance maps of 1952 at the
Library of Congress [[LoC, Sanborn 1952 vol. 3](#sources)], the U.S. Geological Survey's map of
1933–34 for the seven stretches of street joined across the trench [[USGS 1934](#sources)], and
City of Richmond parcel records for Gilpin Court's approximate outline. The pictures are drawn by
Chris Busse with isoscape, the drawing tool he built with Claude (see the [artist
statement](artist-statement.md)). Each pair is cut from the story's views of 1952 and today; the
views of today are drawn from OpenStreetMap and U.S. Geological Survey lidar, as every isoscape
view is [[the story](jackson-ward.md#jackson-ward-today-and-the-proposed-cap-over-i-95)]. The 1952
buildings are traced from the Sanborn sheets, with buildings standing today that the city dates
to 1952 or before; traced ones may sit a few meters from where they stood. Inside today's trench
the 1952 pictures stand on ground rebuilt by estimate, about ±2 m in its middle [[the
story](jackson-ward.md#the-ground-before-the-trench)]. The text was drafted by Claude for Chris
Busse from the published story.

Corrections are welcome through the [contact page](#/about/contact).
