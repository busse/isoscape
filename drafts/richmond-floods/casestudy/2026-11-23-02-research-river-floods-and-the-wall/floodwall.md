# The Richmond Local Flood Protection Project (the floodwall)

All sources read November 23, 2026.

## Answer first

- Richmond's floodwall is a Corps of Engineers project, Norfolk District, built with the City
  under the cost-sharing rules of the Water Resources Development Act of 1986. It has two
  separate parts: a north (Shockoe) wall across the mouth of the Shockoe Valley and a longer
  south (Manchester) line of concrete wall, bin wall and earthen levee. Together they run about
  3.28 mi (17,323 ft) and protect about 750 acres: 150 in Shockoe, 600 in Manchester.
- Construction began November 5, 1988. The wall was dedicated October 21, 1994. City pages say
  "completed in 1995." Both are right in different senses; see below.
- Final cost is given as $143 million. Congress authorized $91.8 million in 1986 and raised the
  ceiling to $134 million in 1993 (75 percent federal).
- There are 19 closures (gates and doors). Partial closures begin when the river reaches about
  9 to 10 ft. The full set has never been closed for a flood.
- The river has not come near the top. The highest water since 1994 was Fran in September 1996,
  23.75 ft at the City Locks (about 22.7 ft NAVD88). The NWS gives 41 ft at the City Locks as the
  stage at which "Flood wall is overtopped," about 5.5 ft above Agnes.
- The design level is stated three different ways in public sources (32 ft, about a 280-year
  flood, a "century flood"). No Corps document giving the design water surface and top-of-wall
  elevation in a stated datum was reachable. UNCONFIRMED.

**1994 or 1995.** The dedication date comes from the marker the Corps and City put up, and the
1993 cost increase shows work still under way then. The 1995 date appears only on later city and
park pages, without detail. I read this as: the wall was dedicated and usable in October 1994 and
final work or formal turnover finished in 1995. The second half is UNCONFIRMED. Use "dedicated
1994" on the plates.

**Cost.** $143 million (City DPU thread; Richmond Family Magazine, July 30, 2019); "more than
$140 million" (VPM); "$140-plus million" (WTVR, 2015). The city now values it at about
$153 million (DPU thread; City FloodKit, https://rva.gov/sites/default/files/2026-03/FloodKit-20260225.pdf).
The final cost split is UNCONFIRMED; the 1993 law sets 75/25.

**Why.** The marker says flooding in the Shockoe Valley and Manchester business districts caused
"serious business losses," cut rail and highway traffic and caused long utility outages. The DPU
thread says Reagan approved it in 1986 after Juan raised the river to 31 ft at the City Locks.
The marker calls it the first project built under the WRDA 1986 cost-sharing provision.

## Design flood and height

Three versions, none with a datum:

1. "Designed to protect Richmond from floods up to 32 feet" (RVANews 2011); the Manchester
   concrete wall "built to withstand a water level of up to 32 feet" (Architecture Richmond,
   https://architecturerichmond.com/inventory/manchester-floodwall-walk/); a WTVR segment titled
   "Richmond floodwall designed to keep out 32 feet of water."
2. Protects against a flood with "an average recurrence interval of about 280 years" (City
   FloodKit, Feb. 25, 2026).
3. "Built to withstand century floods" (12 On Your Side, Sept. 4, 2024).

These cannot all be the same number on the same gauge. Agnes reached 36.5 ft at the City Locks,
so "32 feet" cannot be a Locks stage for a wall meant to stop an Agnes-sized flood. A 280-year
flow would be larger than Agnes (Agnes, 313,000 cfs, sits between FEMA's 1 percent, 281,400, and
0.2 percent, 404,400, flows at Westham). A Westham stage near 32 ft for a 280-year flood is
plausible, so the "32 feet" may be a Westham stage. That reading is mine and UNCONFIRMED. The
"century flood" phrasing is a reporter's and I do not trust it.

Top of wall: no elevation found. The NWS City Locks impact table gives 41 ft (NAVD88 gauge) for
"Flood wall is overtopped, protected area will need to be evacuated"
(https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/ricv2, flood.impacts). Treat 41 ft NAVD88 as the
effective top at the low point of the north wall, pending the Corps record.

## Alignment

**North (Shockoe), about 4,277 ft.** From 12th and Byrd streets to 21st and East Cary streets
(City FAQ). Concrete across the mouth of the Shockoe Valley (FloodKit). Components: 3,947 ft of
concrete wall, 140 ft of sandbag levee up to 7 ft high; 2 railroad, 5 road, 1 railroad and road,
and 4 pedestrian closures; one pump station rated at 75,000 gallons per minute; a pedestrian
overlook and parking lot (marker).

**South (Manchester), about 13,046 ft.** From west of the Manchester (9th Street) Bridge along
the river's edge to the west side of I-95, then south and west, crossing the CSX mainline, to
high ground near Goodes Street (FloodKit; City FAQ). Components: 3,566 ft of concrete wall,
1,887 ft of bin wall and levee, 7,170 ft of earthen levee, 175 ft of sandbag levee; 4 railroad,
1 road and 1 railroad and road closures; two pump stations rated at 4,400 and 28,000 gallons per
minute; the 5,655 ft Floodwall Walk with 3 pedestrian bridges, 2 overlooks and 2 parking lots
(marker; the marker prints the overall length as "13,2046," a typo for 13,046). The walk runs on
top of the wall from the Manchester Bridge area past the Manchester climbing walls (old railroad
bridge piers), over the railroad viaduct, to a lookout near the 14th Street (Mayo) Bridge
(Architecture Richmond). The tallest piece, the gravity U-wall, is just west of the Mayo Bridge.

**Counts reconcile.** The marker's closures add to 6 road, 6 railroad, 2 road and railroad and
4 pedestrian (18). The DPU thread lists 19: the same plus 1 canal closure. Length: marker parts
for the south side sum to 12,798 ft; the DPU thread gives 9,232 ft of Manchester levees (=
7,170 + 1,887 + 175). North plus south (17,323 ft) equals the 3.28 mi in the FloodKit and
Civil Engineering Academy.

## Closures, pumps and interior drainage

- **Closures:** 19 total: 6 roadway, 6 railway, 2 roadway and railway, 1 canal, 4 pedestrian
  (DPU thread). Named ones in sources: the 17th Street closure on Dock Street, the Brander Street
  gate, doors at the Low Line Green "where the trails meet," large floodgates on either side of
  the Mayo Bridge. I found no source using the phrase "magic doors." UNCONFIRMED.
- **When they close:** "barriers are activated in stages," starting at roughly 9 or 10 ft (Bob
  Steidel, city deputy CAO, Dogtown Dish, Sept. 11, 2018,
  https://dogtowndish.com/2018/09/11/will-richmonds-never-tested-floodwall-work); the gauge was not
  named, and the context suggests City Locks. NWS impact stages at City Locks that line up with
  closures: Brander Street closed at 19 ft; Tredegar Street at 23; Mayo Bridge at 24; 21st and
  Cary at 27; Goodes Street at 29. The Friends of James River Park marker at the gauge says the
  Locks reading helps decide "whether to operate the upstream floodwall gate"
  (https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=61821).
- **Pump stations:** north, one at 75,000 gpm; south, two at 4,400 and 28,000 gpm (marker).
  Checked daily, run monthly (City FAQ). Runoff above pump capacity collects in designated
  ponding areas behind the wall and drains by gravity when the river falls (FloodKit). The
  Dock Street pumping station is the one named in Gaston accounts.
- **Shockoe drainage:** Shockoe Creek runs underground in the Shockoe Box (17 by 12 ft) and the
  Shockoe Arch; the 96-inch Shockoe Creek interceptor; the Shockoe retention basin on Chapel
  Island, built 1983, given as 35 or 50 million gallons in different passages
  (https://virginiaplaces.org/waste/csorichmond.html; secondary source). The wall stops the river
  from backing up the valley, but it also holds rain and sewer water in. That is the Gaston
  story (2004); the other agent covers it.
- **A second, separate flood project** protects the city's water filtration plant on the north
  bank about 1.8 mi below the Westham gauge: 150 ft of earthen levee, 2,200 ft of concrete
  floodwall, 5 closures, designed for an estimated 467,000 cfs (City grant application,
  https://dcr.am.virginia.gov/dam-safety-and-floodplains/document/CFPF-21-02-35-CID510129-City-of-Richmond-CFPF-3.pdf).
  In 1989 the Corps had this deferred (HD64). Its construction date is UNCONFIRMED. It belongs on
  any plate that shows the Westham reach.

The gates also close every year for testing (City FAQ; Corps inspects annually). No closure is
reported for February 2003 (Locks 17.76 ft NGVD29, about 16.7 NAVD88) or January 2010, which is
odd beside the 2020 closure at a similar level; the 2020 forecast called for 18.5 ft. The list is
complete only as far as news and city statements go. UNCONFIRMED.

**Has the James reached the wall?** The river reaches the riverside face of the Manchester wall
in ordinary high water, since the wall stands at the river's edge. It has never tested the full
system: "The James never has forced Richmond to close all 19 gates and portals" (RVANews 2011),
and the Mayo Bridge gates have never been needed. The highest water against the wall was Fran,
about 18 ft below the NWS overtopping stage.

## River access

The wall "cut most of Manchester and parts of downtown Richmond off from the river" (Dogtown Dish,
2018). In exchange it carries the Floodwall Walk (5,655 ft) and overlooks, and Floodwall Park at
the south end of the 14th Street Bridge is part of the James River Park System
(https://jamesriverpark.org/?p=12808). Access points cross through the pedestrian closures. The
Low Line Green doors and the Dock Street closure sit on the Canal Walk and Capital Trail routes.
