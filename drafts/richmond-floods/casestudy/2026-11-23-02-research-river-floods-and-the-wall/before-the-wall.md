# Before the wall

All sources read November 23, 2026. This file is thinner than the others: street-by-street
accounts of the old floods were mostly in newspapers and books I could not reach. The best single
source for what went under is a map, USGS HA-410, which shows the 1969 flood.

## Answer first

- Before 1994 the big floods (Camille is the one mapped) put water into the same places, with
  depth rising with the crest: the floor of the Shockoe Valley (Shockoe Bottom up toward Main Street Station), the
  canal and Tredegar ground along the north bank, all of Mayo Island, the Manchester flats between
  the river and roughly Commerce Road (with the sewage treatment plant), Rocketts, and Fulton by
  backwater up Gillies Creek. These are the low, flat tidal-reach districts; the city on the hills
  stayed dry.
- There is no flood-control dam on the James above Richmond. Gathright Dam (1979) controls
  345 sq mi, about 5 percent of the 6,753 sq mi above the Westham gauge.
- Proposals ran from a 1965 Corps flood plain report through the 1980 and 1981 Corps reports to
  authorization in 1986. Agnes (1972) and Juan (1985) are the floods that moved it.
- After the wall, Shockoe Bottom and Shockoe Slip filled with housing and restaurants. That is
  widely said but thinly documented in the sources I read.

## What went under, by district

Source for every 1969 statement: my reading of USGS Hydrologic Atlas HA-410, "Flood of August
1969, Richmond Quadrangle, Richmond, Virginia," E. M. Miller, 1969,
https://pubs.usgs.gov/ha/410/plate-1.pdf (flooded area shaded blue on a 1:24,000 topographic map).

- **Shockoe Bottom.** 1969: the flooded area reaches up the valley floor to about Main Street
  Station and the 17th Street area. 1969 photos: "Main Street flooded" (OFR 70-51 figure 13,
  https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1970/0051/report.pdf); VCU slides of East Main Street and Byrd Street
  (https://scholarscompass.vcu.edu/rcp/index.100.html). 1972: water passed Marshall Street
  (Richmond Family Magazine, July 30, 2019; secondary, UNCONFIRMED). At Dock and Pear streets
  Agnes stood 17 ft deep (High Water Mark sign, https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=133685). 1985:
  "several blocks" of downtown commercial and industrial buildings (Watson,
  https://corpora.tika.apache.org/base/docs/govdocs1/204/204582.html).
- **Shockoe Slip and the canal and tobacco district.** 1969: the canal basin, Tredegar ground and
  the strip under the Lee and Manchester bridges are inside the flooded area on HA-410. 1972:
  3 ft of water at Tredegar Street and Brown's Island Way (https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=124035).
  1936: "serious flooding of industrial and business sections" (Watson).
- **Mayo Island.** Fully under in 1969 (HA-410). Mayo Island and the Mayo Bridge inundated in
  Agnes (James River Association, https://thejamesriver.org/historic-james-river-crests/). NWS
  today closes the Mayo Bridge at 24 ft at the Locks.
- **Manchester.** 1969: the flats from the riverbank to about Commerce Road, around the Seaboard
  and Atlantic Coast Line tracks and including the sewage treatment plant, are flooded (HA-410).
  1972: the water reached 7th Street on the Southside, I-95 on the Southside went under
  (Richmond Family Magazine; secondary), the area including the Richmond Railway Museum building
  was submerged (a museum historian quoted by Dogtown Dish, 2018,
  https://dogtowndish.com/2018/09/11/will-richmonds-never-tested-floodwall-work). The treatment
  plant was the reason the 1986 authorization names "flood protection for the Richmond municipal
  wastewater treatment facility" (WRDA 1986, 100 Stat. 4126).
- **Rocketts.** 1969: a narrow strip along the riverfront below Libby Hill (HA-410). NWS today:
  Rocketts Landing park and the Capital Trail begin to flood at 11 ft at the Locks.
- **Fulton.** 1969: the Gillies Creek valley is flooded from its mouth well up toward Government
  Road (HA-410), backwater from the river rather than creek flow. 1972: "Gillies Creek backed up
  and flooded Fulton" (Richmond Family Magazine; secondary). Fulton was cleared under urban
  renewal in the 1970s; that history is UNCONFIRMED here and belongs to a separate source search.
- **Utilities.** 1972: the water purification plant inundated; VEPCO lost one generating station
  and its dispatch center; downtown lost power, water and communications (NOAA disaster report,
  Oct. 1972,
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CZIC-gb70-9-u55-1972/html/CZIC-gb70-9-u55-1972.htm). Gas
  plant flooded too (NWS, https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood-states-va). About 100,000 people
  without drinking water (Richmond Family Magazine; secondary).
- **Bridges.** 1972: 4 of 5 James bridges closed (NOAA); the 14th Street Bridge underwater; the
  9th Street, Boulevard and Huguenot bridges inaccessible (Richmond Family Magazine). The Boulevard
  Bridge also appears in 1969 flood slides (VCU).
- **1771.** Buildings, boats and animals destroyed in Richmond, about 150 dead along the river
  (https://henrico.gov/locations/flood/). Rocketts was then the port; whether its warehouses were
  lost is UNCONFIRMED.

## Upstream context

- **No mainstem flood-control dam.** The dams above Richmond are low run-of-river structures:
  Bosher's Dam (a diversion dam 1.7 mi above the Westham gauge) and the Williams Island dams that
  divert city water supply (WDR 1986, PDF p. 230). They do not store floods.
- **Gathright Dam's share.** 345 sq mi of drainage (Wikipedia, Gathright Dam) against 6,753 sq mi
  at Westham (USGS site file), about 5 percent. It can trim floods that start in the Jackson River
  valley and does little for storms centered on the Blue Ridge or Piedmont, which made the 1969,
  1972 and 1985 floods. USGS flags every Westham annual peak from 1978 on with code 5,
  "affected to unknown degree by regulation or diversion" (peak file). I found no published
  estimate of how many inches Gathright takes off a Richmond crest. UNCONFIRMED.

## What came after the wall

- Shockoe Bottom and parts of Shockoe Slip were redeveloped, and "thousands more people" now live
  there than before (WTVR, Mark Holmberg, Oct. 2, 2015,
  https://www.wtvr.com/2015/10/02/holmberg-richmonds-floodwall-has-worked-like-a-charm-sort-of).
- The Floodwall Walk, overlooks and Floodwall Park put a public path on top of the Manchester
  wall (Corps/City marker; Friends of James River Park).
- The wall holds water in as well as out: Gaston (2004) flooded Shockoe Bottom from rain and the
  buried creek, with the river low. See the other agent's Gaston file.
- Dates for the Canal Walk, the Manchester apartment conversions and the Rocketts Landing
  development are UNCONFIRMED in this pass.
