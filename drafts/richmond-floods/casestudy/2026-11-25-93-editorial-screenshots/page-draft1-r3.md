The James River has flooded the same low ground in Richmond for as long as anyone has recorded it.
Each great flood drawn here, from 1771 to 1985, put water into Shockoe Bottom and the river streets
below downtown and over Mayo Island; the one that was mapped, in 1969, also covered the Manchester
flats on the south bank [[USGS HA-410](#usgs-ha-410)]. Since 1994 a
floodwall built by the Army Corps of Engineers with the city has kept the river out of Shockoe
Bottom and most of the Manchester flats, and the highest water since then stood about 18
feet below the height at which the weather service says the wall would be overtopped
[[NWS City Locks](#nws-city-locks)]. On August 30, 2004, Shockoe Bottom flooded anyway, from about
10 inches of rain that fell on the land draining to it in one afternoon and evening,
while the James stayed far below flood stage [[Rain analysis](#rain-analysis); [USGS Westham](#usgs-westham)].

The drawings put each flood on the ground it covered. The water is drawn level at the height the
river reached at the City Locks gauge, at the foot of Pear Street, laid on today's lidar survey of
the ground, among the buildings that stood that year as maps, records and air photos show them. They
show where water at that height could stand. They cannot show how the water moved or how long it
stayed, and the section [What the drawings cannot show](#what-the-drawings-cannot-show) sets out
the rest of their limits.

These pictures draw the ground and what stood on it. What the floods meant to the people who lived and
worked in these blocks, and to the families of those who died, is theirs to tell.

## How to read the drawings

::: figure wide
![A close view of the blocks around the 17th Street Market in the 1972 drawing: blue-grey water fills the streets between cream and grey buildings, each building in the water showing a paler band low on its wall, and a small gauge post at the corner of 17th and Main labeled 15 ft](richmond-floods-read.webp)

The blocks around the 17th Street Market in the drawing of June 23, 1972, close up. The gauge at 17th
and Main reads 15 feet: the height the water reached less the height of the ground at that corner
on the lidar. *How this was made:* a crop of the 1972 plate from the series' final draft.
:::

Every flood drawing uses one key. Water standing on land is a blue-grey that darkens with depth,
from a pale edge to its darkest tone at 20 feet and more. Water at its everyday level, in the river and the canal basins, keeps a
light blue, so the river and the ground it covered read apart.
Where water stands against a building, the part of the wall below the surface is drawn over the
water as a paler band, with a dark line at the waterline; it shows best in close views, since on a
whole plate 11 feet of water is about six pixels [[How it's made, Floods](#how-its-made-floods)]. A small post
with a figure is a depth gauge, reading the depth in feet at a named place.

Buildings are drawn as they stood in the year of the flood. A cream building is one that sources
show standing that year: today's buildings dated by records or an air photo, and buildings traced from the Sanborn fire-insurance maps that a later photo still shows. A
grey building with a dashed roof edge appears on an earlier map or photo, and nothing we found says
whether it still stood that year [[How it's made, Floods](#how-its-made-floods)].

A diagonal hatch marks the range of an estimate: ground that water would have reached at the top of
its range. A cross-hatch, used only in Manchester, marks ground whose protection the data cannot
show ([below](#manchester-at-the-1972-height-with-todays-wall-and-without-it)). Every flood also has
a dry twin, the same city and ground drawn with no water and aligned to it pixel for pixel, so a
divider can pass between the two, as in the picture at the top of this page.

Heights on this page are feet above sea level on the national datum surveyors use today (NAVD88),
read at the City Locks gauge unless a place is named. Records made before about 1990 used an older
datum; converted, they come out about 1.03 feet lower at Richmond [[River datums](#river-datums)].

## Why Shockoe Bottom and the Manchester flats flood

Shockoe Bottom is the floor of the valley of Shockoe Creek, which runs south from north of I-64 to
the James near 17th and Dock streets [[The Richmonder 2024](#the-richmonder-2024)]. The city put the creek's
lower course into underground conduits in the 1920s [[Richmond magazine 2013](#richmond-magazine-2013)], so no
stream shows today, but the ground still falls to the same place. On the lidar the lowest point of
the Bottom is a pit beside the 17th Street Market at 13.4 feet above sea level, and the
corner of 17th and Main stands at 21.6 feet [[Spill test](#spill-test)]. Across the river
the Manchester flats run from the riverbank to about Commerce Road [[USGS HA-410](#usgs-ha-410)].

The floods that reach these blocks are made far upstream: the James above Westham drains 6,753
square miles [[USGS Westham](#usgs-westham)], and no dam on the river stores floods before they reach the
city. FEMA's flood study estimates that Gathright Dam, high on the Jackson River, lowers Richmond's
flood peaks by about 1.5 percent [[FEMA Richmond 1998](#fema-richmond-1998)].

Below the falls the James is a tidal river, and in a flood its surface there is close to level. In
1969 the water rose only about 1.3 feet from the City Locks to the Mayo Bridge, then climbed more
than 50 feet through the falls to the Boulevard Bridge [[USGS HA-410](#usgs-ha-410);
[Second read](#second-read)]. Shockoe Bottom, Mayo Island, the Manchester flats and Rocketts all lie on
that flat reach, so one height per flood, read at the gauge, serves all of them.

## The flood of May 1771, drawn as an estimate

The flood of May 1771 is the highest on record at Richmond, and its height rests on diaries. A 1970
report of the U.S. Geological Survey gives a stage of 38 feet for May 27, 1771, from a Corps of
Engineers report, on the scale of the old Weather Bureau gauge near the Mayo Bridge, and says such
early stages were set from authenticated diary accounts [[USGS OFR 70-51](#usgs-ofr-70-51)]. Converted,
that puts the water at about 38 feet at the City Locks, about two and a half feet above Agnes
[[Levels](#levels)]. Other accounts give 30 feet and 45 feet, on bases they do not state
[[Henrico County](#henrico-county); [Richmond Family Magazine 2019](#richmond-family-magazine-2019)]. Henrico County's account of
the flood says it destroyed buildings and boats in Richmond and that about 150 people died along the
river [[Henrico County](#henrico-county)].

::: figure wide
![The Shockoe view with almost no buildings, only the street plan of the 1700s town, and dark water filling the valley floor and the riverfront, with a thin hatched band along its edge](richmond-floods-1771.webp)

Water at about 38 feet, on today's ground, with a hatch to 41 feet for the range. The town of about 1788
is drawn as far as the downtown history can place it: in this frame, six buildings and the street
plan of 1737. *How this was made:* the series' 1771 plate [[Levels](#levels); [Counts](#counts)].
:::

The drawing says where water at that height could stand on the ground as it lies now. It says little
about what the water met in 1771, since few of the town's buildings can be placed, and the ground has
been cut and filled many times since.

## The flood of March 1936

The flood of March 1936 crested at about 26.5 feet at the City Locks [[USGS PP 924](#usgs-pp-924);
[Levels](#levels)]. It is the oldest flood here with surveyed high-water marks: the Geological
Survey plotted about a dozen of them along the river [[USGS HA-410](#usgs-ha-410)]. A National Weather
Service history of Virginia's floods says the water seriously flooded the city's industrial and
business sections [[Watson, Virginia Floods](#watson-virginia-floods)].

::: figure wide
![The Shockoe view in 1936: dark water along the riverfront and up the valley floor to below Main Street, rows of brick buildings and warehouses standing in it](richmond-floods-1936.webp)

March 1936 on the city of 1936: water at 26.5 feet, 159.1 acres under water off the channel, and
144 of the 1,172 buildings shown standing in it [[Counts](#counts)].
:::

## Camille's flood, August 1969

The remnants of Hurricane Camille brought the James to 28.64 feet on the gauge's old datum at 6 a.m.
on August 22, 1969 [[USGS OFR 70-51](#usgs-ofr-70-51)], about 27.6 feet today [[Levels](#levels)]. The
Geological Survey reported that warning ahead of the crest limited losses, though the lowest parts of
the city flooded seriously [[USGS OFR 70-51](#usgs-ofr-70-51)]. It also mapped the flooded ground on its
Richmond map sheet, the best record of any flood here: the floor of the Shockoe Valley up to about
Main Street Station, all of Mayo Island, the Manchester flats as far as about Commerce Road with the
sewage treatment plant, and the Gillies Creek valley through Fulton [[USGS HA-410](#usgs-ha-410)]. FEMA's
flood study records that the levee then standing at the mouth of Shockoe Creek had to stop pumping
during Camille for structural reasons [[FEMA Richmond 2025](#fema-richmond-2025)].

::: figure wide
![The Shockoe view in 1969: water a little higher than in 1936, up the valley floor to about Main Street Station and over Mayo Island, many buildings standing in it](richmond-floods-1969.webp)

August 1969 on the city of 1969: water at 27.6 feet, 174.2 acres under water off the channel, and
141 of the 1,171 buildings shown standing in it. The Geological Survey's mapped water
reached about as far up the valley [[Counts](#counts); [USGS HA-410](#usgs-ha-410)].
:::

## The Agnes flood of June 1972, the highest in the gauge record

Agnes brought the highest water of the gauge era. At the City Locks the James crested at 36.5 feet on
the gauge's datum of the time at 4 p.m. on June 23, 1972 [[NOAA Agnes report](#noaa-agnes-report);
[USGS PP 924](#usgs-pp-924)], 35.5 feet today, and a high-water sign at Dock and Pear streets marks the
same height, 17 feet above the ground there [[Agnes sign](#agnes-sign)]. Upstream at Westham the river
carried 313,000 cubic feet a second, still the record [[USGS Westham](#usgs-westham)].

NOAA's survey of the disaster records that low-lying areas were evacuated from the morning of June 22
and that by June 23 downtown had lost its power and its drinking water. The city's water purification
plant was flooded and four of the five bridges across the James were closed. The National Guard
cordoned off parts of the city [[NOAA Agnes report](#noaa-agnes-report)]. The older levee at the mouth of
Shockoe Creek was overtopped [[FEMA Richmond 2025](#fema-richmond-2025)]. The high-water sign records 13 local
deaths and more than $140 million in local damage [[Agnes sign](#agnes-sign)]; NOAA's report counts five
deaths along the James [[NOAA Agnes report](#noaa-agnes-report)].

In the drawing, water at 35.5 feet covers 220.4 acres of the Shockoe view off the river's
channel, and 226 of the 1,173 buildings shown stand in it, half of them in 10.2 feet
of water or more at their deepest corner, the deepest in 33.2 feet
[[Counts](#counts)]. The gauges read 15 feet at 17th and Main, 11 feet at Main Street
Station and 19 feet at 17th and Dock.

Those 1,173 buildings are today's buildings that records date to 1972 or before, and buildings
traced from the Sanborn maps of the 1950s that a 1974 air photo, or a later one, still shows or does not
rule out. 273 of them are grey: nothing found confirms they still stood that June. The 1974 photo was
taken two years after the flood, so a building it shows gone may have been lost to the flood or
cleared after it. Left out are 40 small outbuildings far from the water and 23 traced footprints lying
under a building of 1972 [[How it's made, Floods](#how-its-made-floods); [Counts](#counts)]. So 226 is a count
of drawn buildings, which comes close to the number that flooded.

## The Election Day flood of November 1985

On November 7, 1985, the James crested at 30.76 feet at the City Locks on the gauge's datum of the
time, about 29.7 feet today [[NWS City Locks](#nws-city-locks); [Levels](#levels)], the second-highest crest
there since gauges began, after Agnes [[River floods research](#river-floods-research)]. The National Weather
Service's flood history says the river flooded several blocks of commercial and industrial buildings
in Richmond [[Watson, Virginia Floods](#watson-virginia-floods)]. The next year Congress authorized the floodwall.

::: figure wide
![The Shockoe view in 1985: water up the valley floor and along the riverfront, a little lower than in 1972, many buildings standing in it](richmond-floods-1985.webp)

November 1985 on the city of 1985: water at 29.7 feet, 192.5 acres under water off the channel,
and 148 of the 1,111 buildings shown standing in it [[Counts](#counts)]. No public air photo
of the 1980s was found, so 180 buildings are drawn grey.

## The floodwall, built from 1988 to 1994

The Corps of Engineers' Norfolk District recommended flood protection for Richmond in a report of
September 1980, and the Chief of Engineers reported on it in November 1981. Congress authorized the
project in the Water Resources Development Act of 1986, signed November 17, 1986, at a cost of $91.8
million, and the law says it "shall include flood protection for the Richmond municipal wastewater
treatment facility" [[WRDA 1986](#wrda-1986)]. The city's utilities department has said the approval
followed the 1985 flood [[City utilities 2020](#city-utilities-2020)]. Construction began on November 5, 1988
[[Virginia HD 64](#virginia-hd-64)]. Congress raised the cost ceiling to $134 million in 1993 [[Public Law 103-135](#public-law-103-135)], and the wall was dedicated on October 21, 1994 [[Floodwall marker](#floodwall-marker)]. The city gives its final cost as $143 million
[[City utilities 2020](#city-utilities-2020)].

It is two separate works. On the north bank a concrete wall about 4,277 feet long runs from 12th and
Byrd streets to 21st and East Cary streets, across the mouth of the Shockoe Valley [[City floodwall FAQ](#city-floodwall-faq)], standing 4 to 30 feet above the ground [[Floodwall marker](#floodwall-marker)]. On the south
bank a line of concrete wall and earthen levee about 13,046 feet long, with a stretch of bin wall,
runs from west of the Manchester Bridge along the river to I-95, then south and west across the railroad to high ground near Goodes
Street [[Floodwall marker](#floodwall-marker); [City floodwall FAQ](#city-floodwall-faq)]. Just west of the Mayo Bridge the
wall is a gravity U-wall, 43 feet from its top to the river's edge [[City floodwall FAQ](#city-floodwall-faq)].
Together the two protect about 750 acres, 150 in Shockoe and 600 in Manchester [[Floodwall marker](#floodwall-marker)]. Streets, rail lines, the canal and footpaths pass through 19 openings, closed with
gates when the river rises [[City utilities 2020](#city-utilities-2020)].

No public document we found gives the wall's surveyed top. The weather service's list of what floods
at each stage says the wall is overtopped at 41 feet at the City Locks [[NWS City Locks](#nws-city-locks)],
about five and a half feet above Agnes, and the drawings use that height for the wall. The city
describes the northern wall as built for a flood with an average recurrence of about 280 years
[[City FloodKit 2026](#city-floodkit-2026)], and FEMA's 1998 study found that the system kept three feet above the
100-year flood and does not protect against the 500-year flood [[FEMA Richmond 1998](#fema-richmond-1998)].

## The wall's two tests: Fran in 1996 and November 2020

The remnants of Hurricane Fran brought the highest water since the wall: 23.75 feet at the City Locks
on September 8, 1996 [[NWS City Locks](#nws-city-locks)], about 22.7 feet today (perhaps a foot more; the record
does not say which datum the gauge used that year) [[Second read](#second-read)], 18 feet below
the overtopping stage. In January 1996 some of the wall's gates were shut, Dock Street's among them,
and in Fran some gates were shut again [[RVANews 2011](#rvanews-2011)].

In November 2020 the James crested at 16.96 feet at the City Locks early on November 14 [[USGS City Locks](#usgs-city-locks)]. The city shut the 17th Street closure on Dock Street at noon on November 12 and the
Brander Street gate in Manchester that evening [[WTVR 2020](#wtvr-2020); [City utilities 2020](#city-utilities-2020)]. The Dock Street closure was shut again in February 2025, for the first time since 2020 [[The Richmonder 2025](#the-richmonder-2025)]. As of 2011 the James had never forced the city to close all of its gates [[RVANews 2011](#rvanews-2011)].

## Manchester at the 1972 height, with today's wall and without it

The south wall is the longer work, and Manchester is where its effect shows most. The divider below
puts water at the height of the 1972 flood, 35.5 feet, on today's Manchester twice: with today's wall
and its gates shut, and with no wall. No flood has done either. Without the wall, 527.1 acres of
this view lie under water and 246 of its 412 buildings stand in it, the deepest in
29.1 feet. At the foot of the Mayo Bridge on Hull Street the water would stand about
14 feet deep. With the wall, the flats behind it are dry, and 41.5 acres lie under water
outside it [[Counts](#counts)].

::: compare wide
![Manchester with today's wall at the 1972 height: the flats dry behind the wall, and a large area at the left, around the treatment plant, drawn with a fine cross-hatch](richmond-floods-manchester-wall.webp) with today's wall
![Manchester with no wall at the 1972 height: dark water across nearly all the flats, around warehouses, tanks and the treatment plant, up to the edge of the higher streets](richmond-floods-manchester-nowall.webp) no wall

Manchester at the 1972 height, with today's wall and its gates shut (left of the divider) and with no
wall (right). The view looks south-southwest from over the river, with the Mayo Bridge at the lower
right. The cross-hatch at the left marks 212.8 acres, with 54 buildings on them, whose
protection the data cannot show [[Counts](#counts)].
:::

The cross-hatch marks the largest thing the drawing does not know. The concrete parts of the south
wall are in OpenStreetMap; the earthen levee, 7,170 feet of it, which carries the line from I-95 south
toward Goodes Street, is not [[Floodwall research](#floodwall-research)]. The lidar shows a continuous ridge
along I-95 at about 43 to 45 feet, above the overtopping stage, but it cannot say whether that ridge is
the levee or the highway's fill [[Case-study log](#case-study-log)]. The 1986 law required the project to
protect the city's wastewater treatment plant [[WRDA 1986](#wrda-1986)], which stands east of I-95. With
the walls alone, the water in the drawing runs round their end into that ground. The drawing does not
guess where the levee runs. It draws that ground cross-hatched, with no depth and out of the counts,
and the hatch's edges follow the levee's possible courses [[How it's made, Floods](#how-its-made-floods)].

## August 30, 2004: Gaston's rain on Shockoe Bottom

Ten years after the wall was dedicated, Shockoe Bottom flooded again, and this time the water fell
from the sky. Gaston came ashore in South Carolina on the morning of August 29, 2004, and was a tropical
depression when its center crossed into Virginia the next day, passing well southeast of Richmond
[[NHC Gaston report](#nhc-gaston-report)]. Storms that formed north and west of the center, along a boundary from
Hampton Roads to Richmond, drifted northwest and stalled over the city from early afternoon into the
evening [[NWS Gaston paper](#nws-gaston-paper)]. The forecasts had called for an inch or two of rain that day
[[NWS Gaston paper](#nws-gaston-paper)].

::: figure wide
![Shockoe Bottom on August 30, 2004: pale blue-grey water in the lowest blocks around the 17th Street Market and Main Street Station, a hatched band around it up the valley, lettered pins A to E at places named in reports, the floodwall in brick red along the riverfront with its openings dashed, and the river low in its channel](richmond-floods-gaston.webp)

Shockoe Bottom in Gaston's rain, on the city of 2004. Solid water to about 28 feet, a middle figure of
the reported depths, and a hatch to 35 feet, the top of what they imply. The floodwall's openings are
drawn dashed, because they stood open. The water is held on the city side of the wall, since with the gates
open and the James low, water that reached the openings ran out to the river. Lettered pins mark
places named in reports [[Gaston depths](#gaston-depths); [Counts](#counts)].
:::

Over the land that drains to the Bottom, about 12 square miles (7,780 acres), the rain
averaged about 10 inches, by the series' own reading of the weather radar corrected to rain
gauges, with a range of 8.6 to 11.6 inches across the estimates [[Rain analysis](#rain-analysis)]. That is about 2.2 billion gallons of water. Most of it fell between 2 and
9 p.m., and every hour from 2 to 8 p.m. brought between 1.3 and 2.2 inches. Its wettest hour, about
2.2 inches from 5 to 6 p.m., was a rain that falls at a point about once in ten years. What was
rare was its length. About 10 inches fell on the whole
watershed in six hours, which reaches or passes the amount NOAA's Atlas 14 gives for a
one-in-1,000-year six-hour rain at a single point, 8.04 inches, by every estimate but one [[Rain analysis](#rain-analysis); [NOAA Atlas 14](#noaa-atlas-14)]. How often a flood like the one that followed may come
depends on the pipes and the ground as well, and is not known.

flash-flood warning went out at 3:58 p.m. [[Style Weekly 2004](#style-weekly-2004)]. The weather service's storm
record starts the flash flooding at 4:30, in a time it labels standard, which may mean 5:30 daylight
time [[Storm Events 2004](#storm-events-2004)]. *How this was made:* the rain panel of the series' Gaston sheet
[[Rain analysis](#rain-analysis)].

The water the drains could not take ran to the low point. Shockoe Bottom is a bowl: its lowest streets
lie below the strip along the river where Dock Street, the railroad embankments and the floodwall stand
[[Gaston mechanism](#gaston-mechanism)]. Runoff poured off Church Hill and the slope below the Capitol into the
Bottom [[AP, Sept. 20, 2004](#ap-sept-20-2004)], and most of the water came together at 17th and Main
[[Washington Post 2004](#washington-post-2004)]. The floodwall's gates stood open [[AP, Sept. 20, 2004](#ap-sept-20-2004)]; the city closes them when the river is forecast to rise [[NBC12 2018](#nbc12-2018)], and
the river stayed low. The James at Westham peaked at 14,800 cubic feet a second at 9:15 p.m., about a
third of the flow at which minor flooding begins there [[USGS Westham](#usgs-westham); [NWS Westham](#nws-westham)].

::: figure wide
![An isometric view of Richmond looking north from across the James, hills drawn three times their height, with the Shockoe Creek watershed outlined in red and arrows running down the valley and off Church Hill to a red dot at the low point of Shockoe Bottom near 17th and Main](richmond-floods-bowl.webp)

The land that drains to one low point near 17th and Main, outlined in red, on isoscape's Richmond of
2004, its hills drawn three times their height to show the bowl. *How this was made:* drawn in code
with Claude over isoscape's drawing [[Rain analysis](#rain-analysis)].
:::

We tested the bowl on the same lidar the drawings use. A pool rising from the Bottom's lowest point
first reaches the James at about 19.1 feet, over a low saddle at 17th and Dock streets; with the wall
standing and its openings open at street level, at about 21.7 feet, through the Dock Street opening
near 18th Street [[Spill test](#spill-test)]. FEMA's model of Shockoe Creek, made for its own purposes,
puts the one-percent flood level near Dock Street at 19.4 feet [[FEMA Richmond 2025](#fema-richmond-2025)]. Every
depth reported in Gaston implies water well above both, so it stood that high only while more of it
arrived than could leave. A rough calculation of water spilling over the riverfront puts the reports
between what an open riverfront and the wall's openings alone would allow, a question for a proper
hydraulic model [[Spill test](#spill-test)]. Whether the wall's line raised the water in the Bottom, and
by how much, is not known.

No surveyed high-water marks were found for Gaston in the Bottom. Reports give depths at single
places: up to 10 feet in the Bottom [[AP, Aug. 31, 2004](#ap-aug-31-2004); [Storm Events 2004](#storm-events-2004)];
water 13 feet up the dining-room wall at Havana '59 on 17th Street, by its owner's account
[[Washington Post 2004](#washington-post-2004)]; 6 feet 2 inches inside Bottoms Up Pizza on Dock Street, above a
3-foot step at its door [[NBC12 2022](#nbc12-2022)]; and 7 feet up the walls of a law office on Main
Street [[AP, Sept. 20, 2004](#ap-sept-20-2004)]. A developer described rapids at 15th and Main [[WTVR 2024](#wtvr-2024)], and the restored Main Street Station escaped damage [[Washington Post 2004](#washington-post-2004)]. Set on the lidar's ground, the depths imply a surface anywhere from about 24 to 35
feet, too wide a spread to call one level [[Gaston depths](#gaston-depths)]. The drawing uses about 28 feet,
the height that "up to 10 feet" at the lowest blocks implies, and hatches the ground above it to 35
feet. At 28 feet the water stands about 9 feet deep at the market and covers 31.4
acres, with 99 of the 1,059 buildings shown standing in it [[Counts](#counts)].

::: figure wide
![A close view of the 17th Street Market in the Gaston drawing: pale blue-grey water in the market and the street, pins A and B, buildings standing in the water, and a hatched band at the right](richmond-floods-gaston-market.webp)

The 17th Street Market in the Gaston drawing, close up: pin A where most of the water came together,
pin B at Havana '59's usual address, and the hatch for the higher reports at the right. *How this was
made:* a crop of the series' Gaston plate.
:::

Eight people died in flash floods in and around Richmond that evening and night, three of them in the
city [[Storm Events 2004](#storm-events-2004); [NHC Gaston report](#nhc-gaston-report)]. Two died in Gillies Creek in the
East End, about a mile east of the Bottom [[AP, Sept. 1, 2004](#ap-sept-1-2004); [Washington Post 2004](#washington-post-2004)]. No source we found places a death in Shockoe Bottom itself. In the Bottom, fire
crews took people out by raft and boat, and at Bottoms Up about two dozen staff and customers waited
on the second-floor deck [[NBC12 2022](#nbc12-2022)]. About 20 blocks were roped off and condemned until
their buildings could be inspected [[Storm Events 2004](#storm-events-2004); [AP, Aug. 31, 2004](#ap-aug-31-2004)], and
the weather service's storm record puts property damage in the city at $20 million [[Storm Events 2004](#storm-events-2004)]. Almost no one in the Bottom carried flood insurance: the wall had taken the Bottom
out of the mapped flood zone, and lenders had stopped requiring it [[AP, Sept. 20, 2004](#ap-sept-20-2004);
[WTVR 2024](#wtvr-2024)].

In August 2009 a group of Shockoe businesses and property owners sued the city, alleging that the way
it ran its sewer gates and a pumping station made the flooding worse; we found no record of how the
case ended [[BizSense 2009](#bizsense-2009)].

Afterward the city moved and added about 100 street inlets and let street water drain into the canal
through flap gates, at a cost of about $20 million over a decade, and in 2018 its utilities director
said the system could carry more than three times the stormwater it could carry in 2004 [[NBC12 2018](#nbc12-2018)]. No rain since has matched Gaston's over the watershed [[Gaston mechanism](#gaston-mechanism)],
and city officials have said that one that did could flood the Bottom again [[NBC12 2018](#nbc12-2018)].

## What the drawings cannot show

- **Movement and time.** Each drawing fills the ground to one height, as water fills a basin. Real
  floods rise and fall, and they reach low ground through drains and culverts that the lidar cannot
  see. Ground below the height that a real flood did not reach in time is drawn wet
  [[How it's made, Floods](#how-its-made-floods)].
- **One height for the whole reach.** The height is read at the City Locks. Toward the Mayo Bridge the
  drawings for 1972, 1985 and 1996 add about a foot, the rise measured in 1969, which nobody measured in
  those floods. West of the bridge the water is held at the bridge's height, while the real surface
  climbs into the falls, so the drawings there are likely low [[Levels](#levels)].
- **Today's ground.** Every flood is drawn on the lidar survey of today's ground, smoothed onto a
  5-meter grid. The ground under the 1771 and 1936 water was not the ground of today
  [[How it's made, Floods](#how-its-made-floods)].
- **Walls the sources do not give.** The floodwall's top is drawn at the weather service's
  overtopping stage, since no surveyed top is public. Manchester's earthen levee is not in the map
  data, so the ground it may protect is cross-hatched. The older levee at the mouth of Shockoe Creek,
  which FEMA says gave limited protection before the floodwall, is not drawn at all: its line and
  height are not in our sources, so the drawings of 1936, 1969 and 1985 may show water where it held
  some back [[FEMA Richmond 2025](#fema-richmond-2025)].
- **Buildings.** The grey buildings are ones no source confirms for the year. Most building heights
  are estimates, as are the heights of bridge decks above the water. A building the water would have
  covered entirely is left off the flood drawing and drawn on its dry twin [[How it's made, Floods](#how-its-made-floods)].
- **Gaston's depths and rain.** The water in the 2004 drawing is a middle figure of news reports, set on
  the lidar; it was moving, and it did not stand at one level. The rain figures are radar estimates
  corrected to gauges, good to about 40 percent either way at a point and better over the whole
  watershed. The watershed is the land surface, which differs from the area the sewers serve [[Rain analysis](#rain-analysis)].

## How this page was made

Chris Busse drew the maps with isoscape, the drawing tool he has built with Claude; see the
[artist statement](artist-statement.md) for how he works with it. The flood levels, the city of each
year, the counts and the rain were worked out in code with Claude under his direction, from the
sources below; the method is in *How it's made*, under [Floods](how-its-made.md#floods-experimental).
The sheets these pictures come from went through an independent inspection and fact check in three
rounds in November 2026, by a Claude agent that had not made them, and wording that said more than

for Chris Busse.
