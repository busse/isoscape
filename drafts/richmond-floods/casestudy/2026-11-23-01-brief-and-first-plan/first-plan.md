# First plan, as given to Chris in chat (2026-11-23, about 18:14)

Drafted by Claude (Fable 5.2) in the planning session from three web searches. Every figure
here is a starting point for the research step, not a checked fact.

## The flood Chris remembered
Tropical Storm Gaston, August 30, 2004. Its remnants stalled over Richmond; nearly a foot of
rain overwhelmed the drainage and pooled in Shockoe Bottom. Press reports said the 1990s wall
was built to keep the James out, and this water came from the sky. Almost nobody in the Bottom
carried flood insurance, the wall having taken the area out of the flood zone. Nineteen
businesses later sued the city over its drains and sewers. Open: whether closed gates or the
wall itself held water in.

## The spine
- Before the wall: at the City Locks gauge, Agnes 1972 (36.5 ft), Juan 1985 (30.76 ft),
  Camille 1969 (28.6 ft). Floods described back to 1816; the 1771 flood on the city's marker.
- The wall: dedicated October 21, 1994; 750 acres protected; north section 4,277 ft
  (150 acres of Shockoe Valley), south section 13,046 ft (600 acres).
- After the wall: Gaston, the flood from inside.

## Why it looked feasible
A marker at Dock and Pear streets gives Agnes as 17 ft above the ground there, 35.5 ft NAVD88:
a surveyed elevation that can be laid on the lidar terrain.

## Risks named
1. Two gauges, two numbers (Westham reads Agnes as 28.62 ft; the Locks 36.5). One datum per flood.
2. A flood surface slopes through the falls; one flat level over the whole view is wrong upstream.

## New capabilities proposed
Flood surface on terrain with depth; the wall as a barrier (same level with and without it);
rain and ponding for Gaston; storm tracks and river hydrographs as context charts.

## Stages proposed
Research (every number sourced) → rough test (Agnes on the downtown view) → Chris's
worth-pursuing call → the set and the story.
