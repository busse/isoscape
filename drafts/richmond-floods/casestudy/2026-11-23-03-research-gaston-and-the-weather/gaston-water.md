# Gaston in Shockoe Bottom: what we need to draw the water

Source keys resolve in `sources.md`. All sources read November 23, 2026.

## Bottom line for the drawing

**Only depths at places can be supported, not a single water-surface elevation.** No surveyed
high-water marks, no official inundation map and no water-level gauge for Gaston in Shockoe Bottom
turned up. The reported depths come from news accounts of single buildings, and when set on ground
elevations they do not agree on one level: they imply a surface somewhere between about 27 and 35
ft NAVD88 (my calculation below). The water was also moving, with "rapids" reported on Main Street
[S33], so a flat pool is itself an approximation. If the series must draw a level, I would draw
about **28 to 30 ft NAVD88 over the lowest blocks, labeled approximate**, and say the depths are
reported, not surveyed. That choice rests on the most repeated figure ("up to 10 feet") applied at
the low point. It is a judgment, not a measurement.

## 1. Extent: which blocks

- "About 20 blocks" of downtown were closed, roped off or condemned [S5 event 5421493], [S24],
  [S25], [S27]; AP called it about half the historic area, near the river [S24], [S27]. No source
  lists the blocks or prints a cordon map. UNCONFIRMED which 20.
- Named places with water: 17th Street and the Farmers' Market [S23], [S28]; 17th and Main, said to
  be where most water converged [S28]; 15th and Main ("rapids") [S33]; East Main and North 17th
  (photo caption) [S40 p42]; a Main Street law office [S22]; Dock Street at Bottoms Up Pizza [S31];
  "across from the train station," where two buildings were later demolished [S28].
- Named place without water: Main Street Station "escaped damage" [S28].
- Outside the Bottom (separate flooding, not one pool): Gillies Creek in the east end [S24], [S28];
  Church Hill slides and the sinkhole at East Grace and 31st [S23], [S34]; Acca Yard (CSX) [S23].
- The city's 2023 Shockoe plan maps FEMA flood hazard areas (100- and 500-year floodplain and a new
  floodway) for the district [S40 p42-43, Figure 12]. That is a regulatory map, not a Gaston
  inundation map, but it is the nearest official polygon.

## 3. Ground elevations (spot checks)

I queried the USGS 3DEP Elevation Point Query Service [S41] at approximate coordinates. These are
rough: I picked the coordinates from the map, not from surveyed addresses, and a point a few meters
off can land on a curb, a wall or a building footprint edge. The isoscape lidar should replace
them.

| Point (approximate) | Lat, lon | Ground, ft NAVD88 |
|---|---|---|
| 17th and East Main | 37.53386, -77.43040 | 21.7 |
| 18th and East Main | 37.53320, -77.42910 | 16.7 |
| 15th and East Main | 37.53250, -77.43220 | 21.2 |
| Cary and 17th | 37.53290, -77.43150 | 20.8 |
| 17th and Dock (floodwall marker coordinates [S37]) | 37.53218, -77.42977 | 21.7 |
| Dock and 18th | 37.53150, -77.42850 | 21.4 |
| 17th and Franklin | 37.53470, -77.43120 | 33.7 |

A coarse grid at roughly 70 m by 90 m spacing over the Bottom [S41, my queries] found the lowest
ground at about **17 to 18 ft** in the blocks around Main and Cary between 17th and 19th streets
(for example 17.5 ft at 37.5324, -77.4290). The strip toward the river along Dock Street, the
railroad and the floodwall read about 20 to 28 ft; water surfaces in the canal and river read
about 0.4 to 1.5 ft (on the lidar's flight date, not 2004).

**The low point of Shockoe Bottom**: on these spot checks, about 17 ft NAVD88 near 18th and Main.
The Washington Post names 17th and Main as the lowest point on the downtown side [S28]. These agree
within a block. Confirm with the lidar.

## 4. What the depths imply for a water surface (my calculation)

| Report | Ground used | Implied surface, ft NAVD88 |
|---|---|---|
| "Up to 10 ft" at the low point | 17 to 18 | 27 to 28 |
| Bottoms Up, about 9 ft above Dock Street | about 21.4 | about 30 to 31 |
| Havana '59, 13 ft on 17th Street | about 21 to 22 | about 34 to 35 |
| Law office, 7 ft on Main Street | about 17 to 22 (exact block unknown) | about 24 to 29 |

The spread, about 24 to 35 ft, is too wide to call one level. Plausible reasons: depths measured
from different floors, the 13-ft figure being a splash or wave mark or a below-grade room, water
sloping downhill toward the low point, and my approximate ground heights. The best-supported single
statement remains depth at places.

Best-supported surface, if one is needed: **about 28 ft NAVD88, uncertainty at least plus or minus
3 ft**, from "up to 10 feet" at the low point, the most repeated report. UNCONFIRMED as a surveyed
value.

## 5. Surveyed high-water marks and inundation maps

- USGS: I found no Gaston high-water marks for Richmond. UNCONFIRMED; the USGS Flood Event Viewer
  (STN) was not checked event by event.
- City of Richmond: no inundation map found. The DPU and its consultants may hold one (the 2006
  study modeled the downtown sub-basin [S16]; the 2022 Greeley and Hansen floodplain study modeled
  the Shockoe floodplain [S40 p42, p46]). Neither is public that I found.
- Insurance or FEMA claims data by block: not public at that resolution.
- Photographs: many exist (news and the city plan [S40 p42]). Photo-based depth reading against
  known building features is the most promising route to more depths. Not done here.

## 6. Which way the water moved

- Off the slopes: runoff ran off Church Hill and the Capitol slope into the Bottom [S22].
- Down the valley: the buried Shockoe Creek runs south from north of I-64 to 17th and Dock [S35];
  the floodwater followed the old creek channel toward the river [S36].
- Through the streets: "rapids" at 15th and Main [S33]; cars swept through the streets and into
  buildings [S24], [S25]; a bus half-submerged at an intersection [S25].
- On Church Hill: a rush of water down Broad Street toward the ravine near the tunnel [S34].
- To the low point: most water converged on 17th and Main [S28].
- Out: the water left by the sewers and over time; one man waited four and a half hours for it to
  go down [S22]. Whether surface water spilled over the riverfront line (Dock Street, the railroad,
  the floodwall) or through the open closures is UNCONFIRMED. One witness described water "coming
  over the wall near the train station" [S33]; the wall is not identified.

## 7. What to draw, in order of confidence

1. Rain over the whole Shockoe valley and the city (high confidence; see `rainfall-data.md`).
2. The James low and inside its banks, with the floodwall openings open (high confidence) [S10],
   [S12], [S22], [S30].
3. Water standing in the lowest blocks of the Bottom, deepest near 17th-18th and Main and Dock,
   "up to about 10 feet" (moderate confidence on depth, low on exact edges).
4. Edges of the pool: draw them by contour at the chosen level from the lidar, and say so
   (low confidence).
5. Flow arrows down the valley and off Church Hill (moderate).
6. Separate damage sites outside the pool: Gillies Creek deaths, Church Hill slide and sinkhole
   (high confidence on place, not on water extent).

The single biggest risk: drawing a crisp, level flood edge that looks surveyed. The depths are
anecdotal and disagree by several feet.
