# Tropical Storm Gaston, August 29-31, 2004: timeline

Source keys resolve in `sources.md`. All sources read November 23, 2026. Times are Eastern
Daylight Time (EDT, UTC minus 4) unless marked UTC.

**In one paragraph.** Gaston came ashore in South Carolina as a minimal hurricane on the morning
of Sunday, August 29, 2004, and was a weak tropical depression by the time its center crossed into
Virginia on Monday, August 30. Its center passed well southeast of Richmond. North and west of the
center, thunderstorms formed on a boundary running from Hampton Roads to Richmond, drifted
northwest, slowed and stalled over the city from early afternoon to late evening. Six to more than
twelve inches of rain fell around Richmond in about eight hours. Shockoe Bottom, the low ground
where the buried Shockoe Creek meets the James, filled with water up to about 10 feet deep. Eight
people died in flash floods in the Richmond area. The James River itself rose only modestly and
never came near flood stage.

The forecasts missed the rain. Both the NAM and GFS called for 1 to 2 inches over southern Virginia
for the day [S3 p1]; another paper says the HPC and NAM forecasts were an inch or less [S4]. AP
reported that forecasters had expected no more than 4 inches [S23], [S25]. The NWS Wakefield
warning coordination meteorologist said forecasters caught the storm but not its rainfall
intensity [S20].

## 2. Why Richmond: the meteorology in brief

- By 11 a.m. (1500 UTC) convection was forming along a surface boundary from near Hampton to
  Richmond; the storms moved northwest and slowed [S3 p4].
- By 2 p.m. (1800 UTC) cloud tops over Richmond had cooled to about minus 70 C [S3 p4].
- Radar showed reflectivity of at least 35 dBZ over Richmond for six straight hours; the
  strongest returns formed just south of Richmond around noon and lasted to about 4 p.m.
  (1600-2000 UTC) [S4]. CAPE was just over 1,900 J/kg, with strong low-level shear [S4].
- At 5 p.m. (2100 UTC) the heaviest one-hour rain on the Wakefield radar was centered over
  Richmond and stayed there for several hours [S3 p4-5].
- Style Weekly's account: a patch of wind held the densest part of the storm over the city, and a
  sunny morning had left the air unstable [S20].

Unit check for S8: the airport's three-hour total ending 4:54 p.m. was 100.3 mm (3.95 in), and its
six-hour total ending 8 p.m. was 140.0 mm (5.51 in) [S8, AA2 fields at 2004-08-30T20:54 and
2004-08-31T00:00 UTC]. The airport's day total, 169.7 mm, matches the official 6.68 in [S8, SOD
record], [S1 p8].

Radar rates over Shockoe: the NWS flash-flood monitoring display (FFMP) for a sub-basin near
Shockoe Bottom at 5:58 p.m. (2158 UTC) showed several peaks above 3 inches per hour; the same paper
also describes the rate as 2 to 3 inches per hour [S3 p5, Fig. 18]. The two figures are not
reconciled in the paper. Flash-flood guidance for the Richmond sub-basin had been lowered to about
1 inch per hour [S3 p5].

Events in order (sources in brackets; news items are attributed reports, not measurements):

- **About 11:24 a.m.**: first trace of rain at the airport [S8, 1524 UTC special report].
- **About 1:30 p.m.**: water began seeping into a basement apartment, according to its tenants
  [S24], [S26]. AP does not give the address; the story is about Shockoe Bottom. UNCONFIRMED that
  the apartment was in the Bottom.
- **2 to 4 p.m.**: the most intense rain at the airport (1.27 and 2.10 in. in successive hours)
  [S8].
- **3:58 p.m.**: NWS Wakefield issued the first flash-flood warning for Richmond, per its warning
  coordination meteorologist, Bill Sammler [S20]. The NWS paper says warnings went out more than
  90 minutes before the worst flooding [S3 p1], which puts the worst flooding after about 5:30 p.m.
- **By 4:30 p.m.**: flooding had begun in Shockoe Bottom, according to the 2009 lawsuit, which
  alleges the city opened sewer gates only after 4:30 p.m. [S18]. This is an allegation.
- **4:30 p.m.**: NCEI Storm Events start time for the flash floods in the city of Richmond, Henrico
  and Chesterfield; Hanover at 5:00 p.m. [S5, events 5421493, 5421492, 5421490, 5421491]. The file
  labels these times "EST". Whether they are standard or daylight time is UNCONFIRMED; if standard,
  add an hour.
- **5 p.m. onward**: heaviest one-hour radar rain centered over the city for several hours [S3 p4-5];
  AORC puts 1.0 to 1.6 inches per hour over the Bottom every hour from 2 p.m. to 8 p.m. [S14].
- **About 6:30 p.m.**: an IT worker leaving his office in the Bottom saw a man spinning in a
  floating BMW [S23].
- **Evening**: water rose in under an hour [S31], [S32]. At Bottoms Up Pizza on Dock Street the
  kitchen was nearly under water in under 15 minutes and about two dozen staff and customers went
  up to the second-floor deck [S31]; about 20 people were stranded there [S25]. A marooned bus had
  passengers lifted out through its windows [S27]. A brick building of at least two stories
  collapsed onto cars [S24], [S25]. A substation feeding the Bottom was destroyed [S23]. Fire crews
  took people out by raft and boat [S31], [S33]; a cook was taken by boat from Bottoms Up "Monday
  night" [S26]. Police told callers to stay where they were rather than send cars [S33].
- **"Monday night"**: Gov. Mark Warner declared a state of emergency [S23]. (Wikipedia gives
  Aug. 31 [S43]; AP's "Monday night" is the earlier and more specific account.)
- **9:15 p.m.**: the James at Westham, above the falls, peaked at 14,800 cubic feet per second,
  having started the day near 2,000 [S10]. The NWS minor-flood flow there is 42,000 cfs (12 ft)
  [S12]. The river never approached flood.
- **10:00 p.m.**: Storm Events end time for the city of Richmond and Henrico flash floods [S5].
- **About 10:54 p.m.**: rain ended at the airport [S8].
- **Recession**: a patent lawyer in a Main Street office stood in a niche inside his door for four
  and a half hours until the water went down [S22]. No measured recession times exist that I found.
- **Tuesday, Aug. 31**: about 20 blocks of downtown were roped off and declared uninhabitable [S25];
  AP called it about half the historic area [S24], [S27]. Storm Events says about 20 blocks were
  condemned [S5, event 5421493]. City Manager Calvin Jamison gave an early estimate of more than
  $15 million in infrastructure damage [S24].
- **Wednesday, Sept. 1**: a woman's body was found in woods east of the city, after her car stalled
  in floodwater and she was swept away [S24].
- **Friday, Sept. 3**: President Bush declared a major disaster, FEMA DR-1544 [S15].
- **Late that week**: owners and residents could re-enter only with escorts until buildings were
  inspected [S22].
- **The following week**: bulldozers were still clearing sludge [S20].

## 4. Places

Shockoe Bottom (all from news unless noted):

- **17th Street and the Farmers' Market**: the open-air market was swamped [S28]; cars floated into
  it [S23]. Most water converged on 17th and Main, which the Post calls the lowest point on the
  downtown side of the river [S28]. Havana '59 on 17th Street: water 13 feet up the dining-room wall
  [S28].
- **East Main Street**: a patent lawyer's office on Main Street had water 7 feet up its walls; at
  one point water stood 2 feet deep outside his door, pushing in [S22]. The city's Shockoe plan
  prints a Gaston photo captioned at East Main and North 17th [S40 p42]. A developer described
  rapids at 15th and Main [S33].
- **Main Street Station**: the restored depot escaped damage [S28]. A developer described water
  coming over "the wall near the train station" [S33]; which wall is not said. Two buildings across
  from the station were later demolished [S28].
- **Dock Street**: Bottoms Up Pizza, 6 ft 2 in of water inside, above a 3-foot step at the entrance
  [S31]; owner's damage over $1.5 million [S31].
- **I-95 and the railroad viaducts**: CSX rail through the city was shut and its Acca Yard stayed
  under water [S23]; I-95 and more than 100 regional roads were closed [S23]. No source I read
  describes water under the I-95 or railroad viaducts in the Bottom specifically. UNCONFIRMED.
- **Church Hill slopes**: runoff poured off Church Hill and the Capitol slope into the Bottom [S22].
  A mudslide tore the face off a 30-foot hill and trapped cars in 3 feet of mud [S23]. At East
  Grace and 31st streets the ground dropped about 20 feet into a sinkhole about an acre across,
  likely fill over an old ravine near the Church Hill tunnel; FEMA paid about $3.5 million toward
  repairs including a 200-foot retaining wall [S34]. (WTVR dates the collapse Aug. 29, which is
  wrong; the rain fell Aug. 30.)
- **Gillies Creek** (east end): two people died in the creek, possibly in a human chain reaching for
  people in a van [S24], [S26]; the Post puts it about a mile east of the Bottom [S28].
- **Battery Park** (North Side): I found no source describing Gaston flooding there. UNCONFIRMED.

Region:

- **Chesterfield**: a woman died in a submerged car [S24]; police and fire rescued about 40 people
  overnight [S25]; Falling Creek overtopped a dam and hundreds were evacuated (Wikipedia, citing a
  regional hazard mitigation plan I could not open) [S43]. Powhite Parkway was submerged [S33].
- **Hanover**: two deaths [S24]. Ashland airport gauge 10.61 in; Mechanicsville 10.70 in [S1 p8].
- **Henrico**: one death [S5, S6]; Sandston 8.10 in [S1 p8], [S9].
- **King William**: 6 to 14 inches in the north of the county [S5, event 5421494]; AP reported 14
  inches [S23].
- **Appomattox River at Matoaca**: crested 13.57 ft on Aug. 31, moderate flood (flood stage 10)
  [S53].

How the published numbers arise:
- **5**: AP on Aug. 31 and Sept. 1 (two Richmond, one Chesterfield, two Hanover) [S23], [S25], [S27].
- **6**: AP on Sept. 1 adds the woman found east of the city [S24].
- **8**: NHC, NWS and Storm Events direct deaths [S1 p3], [S3 p1], [S5]. NHC says five were
  motorists (one drove around a barricade) and three died in rescue attempts [S1 p3].
- **9**: 8 direct plus 1 indirect [S1 p3]; NBC12 uses 9 [S31], [S32].

The series should say: eight people died in flash floods in and around Richmond, three of them in
the city. No source I read places a death inside Shockoe Bottom. UNCONFIRMED either way.

## 7. The cordon and the disaster declaration

- **Cordon**: about 20 blocks of Shockoe Bottom closed until buildings were inspected [S24], [S25],
  [S27]; escorted re-entry only until late that week [S22]. Almost no one in the Bottom had flood
  insurance; lenders had stopped requiring it [S22], and the floodwall had taken the Bottom out of
  the mapped flood zone [S33].
- **State**: state of emergency declared Monday night, Aug. 30 [S23].
- **Federal**: FEMA DR-1544, "Severe Storms, Flooding and Tornadoes Associated with Tropical
  Depression Gaston," declared Sept. 3, 2004; incident period Aug. 30 to Sept. 8 [S15]. Designated:
  the cities of Richmond, Colonial Heights, Hopewell and Petersburg, and the counties of Charles
  City, Chesterfield, Dinwiddie, Hanover, Henrico, King William, New Kent and Prince George (12)
  [S15, OpenFEMA]. Individual Assistance approved $9,262,094.78 (3,296 applications approved);
  Public Assistance obligated $20,783,468.17 [S15]. Public Assistance went to Richmond,
  Chesterfield, Colonial Heights, Hanover, Henrico, King William and New Kent [S15, OpenFEMA].
- **Recovery**: local fundraising reached about $350,000 by late 2004 [S17], [S28]; one restaurant
  reopened after 11 months with a $57,000 city grant [S17].
