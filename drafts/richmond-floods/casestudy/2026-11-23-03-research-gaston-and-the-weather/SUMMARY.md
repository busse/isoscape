# Gaston (August 30, 2004) and the weather data: research summary

Written by an Opus 5.5 subagent, filed by the planning session. Source keys like [S1] resolve
in `sources.md`. All sources read November 23, 2026. Not yet checked by a second reader.

## The straight answer to "did the floodwall trap the water?"
Partly. The popular telling gets the gates wrong.
- The water was rain. 8 to 12 inches fell on the Shockoe Creek valley in about eight hours
  [S1 p3]. The James stayed low: 14,800 cfs at peak; minor flooding starts at 42,000 [S10], [S12].
- The floodwall never closed. Its gates stood open [S22], [S29], [S30].
- The drains could not carry the water: the buried creek sewers (the Arch and the Box), the
  street inlets and the Dock Street pumps [S3 p1], [S16], [S17], [S30].
- "Trapped" is right in one sense only. The Bottom is a bowl: its river side (Dock Street,
  the railroad embankments and the wall) stands higher than its lowest streets. No source
  measures how much of that barrier is the wall rather than the older embankments.
- The real dispute is about sewer gates. A 2009 suit by 18 or 19 businesses alleged a sewer
  gate rusted shut, gates opened late, a pumping station without power and a retention basin
  already full [S18], [S19]. No ruling, settlement or dismissal found.

## Stated with confidence
Monday, August 30, 2004; heavy rain about 2 to 9 p.m. 6.68 inches at the airport; two
Richmond reports over 12 inches. Water up to about 10 feet deep in the lowest blocks; about
20 blocks closed. Eight direct deaths in the region and one indirect (Richmond 3, Hanover 2,
Chesterfield 1, Henrico 1, Dinwiddie 1). FEMA DR-1544, September 3, 2004, 12 localities.
About $20 million spent afterward on inlets and sewers.

## Shaky
- One water level: reported depths imply anything from about 24 to 35 ft NAVD88 (the agent's
  arithmetic). Only depths at named places hold up.
- Rain on the watershed itself: no gauge stood there.
- UNCONFIRMED: whether the wall raised the flood at all; pump station power; the rusted gate;
  the retention basin (35 million gallons per the city, 44 or 50 elsewhere).
- "10 to 12 feet", "14 inches in five hours" and "1,000-year flood" are not supported as stated.

## Biggest risk in drawing it
A crisp, level flood edge that looks surveyed, with the wall shown holding the water in.
Draw instead: depths at named places, a soft pool around the low point near 17th to 18th
and Main, the river low beside it, the wall's gates open.
