# Richmond floods from rain inside the city, before and after Gaston

Source keys resolve in `sources.md`. All sources read November 23, 2026.

## The answer

**Gaston was unique in scale, not in kind.** Shockoe Bottom floods from rain on its own valley
often, in small ways: the federal storm record notes rain flooding there in 2000, twice in 2004,
in 2006, 2018, 2019 and 2024 [S54]. Gaston is the only one in that record with deep water, deaths in
the city, condemned blocks and a federal disaster declaration [S5], [S15]. Before 1996 the federal
database holds no flood events at all, so older rain floods have to come from newspapers and city
records, which I did not search systematically.

filter]. Reports depend on someone calling them in, so absence is weak evidence.

## Rain floods elsewhere in the city (selected, Storm Events)

- July 19, 2000: storm drains on Maury Street overwhelmed; up to 5 ft of water in the basement of
  the DPU's field operations building [S54 event 5155273].
- Sept. 16, 1999 (Floyd): flash flooding in the city, $176,000 [S54 event 5720260].
- July 22, 2005: Gillies Creek over its banks in the East End [S54 event 5470696].
- Aug. 5, 2020: Mosby Street flash flood; about 12 people rescued from cars and a store; Church
  Hill streets impassable [S54 events 917650, 917651].
- Recurring trouble spots in 2018-2026: Belvidere and Canal streets; Bainbridge at 19th to 21st
  (Manchester), with a boat rescue on July 12, 2025; the Magnolia Street underpass; Mosby Street;
  I-95 under Broad Street; Chamberlayne and Laburnum [S54, events listed by date in the 2018-2026
  files].
- June 28, 2026: a restaurant closed by water inside; a car submerged; about 3 ft of water on an
  I-95 ramp to the Downtown Expressway [S54 events 1353001, 1353004, 1352995].
- 2025 was a heavy year for warnings: 68 flash-flood warnings in metro Richmond by mid-July, 14 for
  the city [S51].

## Before 1996

- Shockoe Creek floods were the reason for its enclosure: earthen berms at its mouth around 1900, and
  a 1923-1928 project that buried the creek [S36]. Dates of the floods that prompted these:
  UNCONFIRMED.
- A 1968 suit by Richmond Engineering Company over flood damage, settled with a city duty to keep a
  detention basin along I-64 clear, is mentioned in the 2009 lawsuit [S18]. Its flood date and cause:
  UNCONFIRMED.
- Connie (Aug. 12, 1955) set the airport's one-day record, 8.79 in [S2], [S55]. What it did in the
  Bottom: not researched. UNCONFIRMED.
- The city's Shockoe plan says Camille (1969), Agnes (1972) and Gaston (2004) each caused
  catastrophic damage in Shockoe [S40 p25, timeline; also p88]. Camille and Agnes were mainly James River
  floods (covered by the river research), though both also brought rain to the city: 2.69 in for
  Camille, 6.92 in for Agnes over June 19-21 [S52].

## What the series can say

"Shockoe Bottom has flooded from its own rain many times, mostly a foot or two in the streets.
Gaston is the only time in the modern record that it stood up to about ten feet deep." The second
sentence rests on the record since 1996 and on the depths in `gaston-water.md`; before 1996 it is
UNCONFIRMED.
