Edge-of-town test: Marion, South Hill, Chase City

Research draft made with Claude, November 21, 2026. Three towns chosen to test the measure, not as a sample. Ten Dollar General stores, one Family Dollar and two Dollar Tree stores sit within 8 km of these three towns' limits. Nothing here is a finding about Virginia.

What the maps show

  • Six of the ten Dollar General stores sit inside the built-up area on the loose edge (score A at or below 1). Four sit far beyond it (A from 3.1 to 6.0), on US and state routes 8 to 9 km from the town's center. One of them, on Lee Highway east of Marion, is off the map's 16 km square. None falls in the fringe band from 1 to 2.
  • Supermarkets and pharmacies are all inside the town limits. Convenience stores spread out more like the Dollar General stores: 8 inside the loose edge, 5 in the fringe, 9 beyond.
  • So in these towns the picture is "in town, or out on the road between towns". The edge itself is empty. Twenty towns will say whether that holds.

What the test says about the measures

  1. Strict edge (425 homes per sq mi) fails in Marion. Its built-up piece is 5.2 km2 and leaves out the North Main Street strip, so Food City, Food Lion, Ingles, Walmart and CVS all score 2.8 to 5.2, "beyond the edge". Homes thin out where shops begin; a housing-only edge calls every commercial strip "outside town".
  2. Loose edge (200 homes per sq mi) reads right in all three. Marion's strip falls inside (groceries 0.4 to 0.6). South Hill's I-85 interchange cluster (Walmart, Dollar Tree) still scores 1.4 to 1.5, which matches how it looks on the ground: a highway cluster just outside the old town.
  3. The Census itself adds commercial land. Its 2020 urban areas start from blocks with 425 homes per square mile or 20 percent impervious cover, then fill with blocks at 200. Adding impervious cover (NLCD, public domain) to the loose edge would bring commercial strips in by the Census's own rule. Recommended.
  4. Measure B is clear on a map but coarse. It sorts stores into inside the limits (5 of 10 Dollar General), urban area outside the limits (2), and outside both (3). Chase City (2,053 people) has no urban area at all, so B is only "inside or outside the limits" there. Virginia's limits also include empty land: South Hill's limits are 25.6 km2 around a 9.9 km2 built-up area.
  5. The random-road comparison needs a tighter window. Over the full 8 km square, 80 percent of main-road frontage scores beyond 2, so the comparison mostly says "stores are in towns". To test edge against center, the next run compares within score 3 of each town, and gives the "between towns" stores their own class.
  6. Data checks. Every Dollar General, Family Dollar and Dollar Tree mapped in OpenStreetMap here lies within 76 m of its SNAP point, except one at 448 m in South Hill (to check). OSM has 7 of the 13 chain stores; SNAP stays the primary source.

Proposal

Lead with A on the loose edge plus impervious cover, drawn as a blue built-up outline. Draw the town limits as the black line (B) on every map. Report three classes in the charts: in town (A at or below 1), fringe (1 to 2), between towns (above 2).
