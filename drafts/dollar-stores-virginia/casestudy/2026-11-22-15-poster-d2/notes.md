# Step 15: editorial poster, working draft d2 (November 22, 11:25-11:50)

Chris, 11:25, on the d1 proof: "My general feedback on the proof review is that some copy still
shows telltale signs of AI writing, ex: 'How we did this, and what it can't us'"

## What was rewritten, and why

The tells, and the fix for each:

- **Paired, balanced headings.** "How we did this, and what it can't tell us" became two
  separate column heads: "How the edge was measured" and "What the data can't show".
- **A set of three matching question headings.** "Is it true?", "Where are the ones at the
  edge?" and "When did they come?" became plain labels of different shapes: "Where the stores
  are", "What the edge stores sit next to" and "How new the stores are".
- **A tidy closing line.** "Because they stand on the main roads into town, they are easy to
  notice" became a hedged plain sentence: "These are the roads people use to get into town,
  which may be why the edge stores seem so common."
- **Abstract nouns.** "Follow the same pattern" became "do the same thing" and "land in nearly
  the same places". "Fit none of the patterns" became "the ones that don't fit".
- **Stiff phrasing.** "To check it fairly", "Suppose the stores had been placed" and "It says
  nothing about the quality of any store or town" were replaced with plainer wording: "To
  check it wasn't a fluke", "If the stores had been dropped at random spots", and "It is not a
  judgment of any store or town".
- **Concrete numbers in the deck.** "Within 800 feet" in place of "near". "Compared with 50% of
  the stores in town" now gives the reader the comparison.
- **"Gas stations" removed** from r1's deck. The data has convenience stores and does not say
  how many sell gas.
