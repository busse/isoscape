# Plan: Tutorial 2, after Chris's decisions (November 25, 2026)

**Working title:** "Tutorial: draw a neighborhood's history from old maps and open data with an AI
chat, using Jackson Ward in Richmond". Descriptive, as for every title.

**Showcase:** the two story pages, *Jackson Ward, Richmond, VA, before I-95, block by block* and
*Redlining in Richmond, VA: the 1937 federal mortgage map* (Redlining's D1 includes Jackson Ward).

**New worked example, made fresh, with the real prompts recorded:** the four corners of West
Broad and North Belvidere streets, in 1925 and 1952 (Sanborn, Library of Congress) and today.
Outside isoscape's existing traces, so the tutorial shows a sheet fitted and traced from
scratch. VCU's growth there is a live debate. Treat it evenly: the drawing and the counts, and
on each side what the people involved have said, with sources.

**One page.** Sections:
1. What a story page is: one question worked out with drawings, counts and sources (the two stories).
2. Choose a question a map can answer.
3. Find the historical layers and check each: Sanborn (LoC), HOLC map (National Archives via
   FRASER, Mapping Inequality), the assessor's year-built, lidar. The five questions from Tutorial 1.
4. Put an old map on today's ground: street corners, the fit, how far each corner misses.
5. Trace it in code and check the trace against the sheet; the survivorship trap.
6. Rebuild what's gone, and say how far off it may be (the ground before the trench).
7. Count with a written rule: polygon, rule, measured / inferred / unknown.
8. Draw each state and compare (sliders).
9. Write it so it can be checked: answer first, a source for each fact, "How we know",
   corrections, who keeps the history; the PDFs and the social preview.
10. Care: lived history, outside review or say plainly there was none, credit the local keepers, no prints.
11. Make your own: one Sanborn sheet, two editions, one block; an honest note on effort.

**Reader:** the Claude desktop app, no programming; Tutorial 1 comes first.
**Stages:** research → outline (Chris approves) → draft and fact check → local preview → the
planning session publishes. Board: a `site` entry.
