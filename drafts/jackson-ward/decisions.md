# Jackson Ward story: the owner's decisions on the outline

Chris Busse, November 18, 2026, answering the outline's eight questions (outline.md, section 7).
Recorded with Claude.

1. **Title:** "What stood where the highway runs".
2. **1925:** yes, now, as a fourth state, knowing it may be cut later. The story is editorial
   and educational, and 1925 is likely an important epoch.
3. **Gilpin Court:** gets its own beat (the 1941 clearance of Apostle Town, the 1957 and 1970
   additions, today's replacement plan).
4. **Reading order:** chronological, starting with the earliest state. A book might later open
   with today ("tell them what you're going to tell them"); the explorer story does not.
5. **The cap:** the study area drawn over today's ward and the range of views, each sourced,
   with no position of our own.
6. **Where it lives:** a story page in the explorer, linked from the Jackson Ward views.
7. **Reviewers:** they see it as complete as possible. The story goes through its fact check
   first; the community reviewers get the fact-checked draft (the "outside review" stage).
   Sharing it is the owner's to do. Nothing is published before their review.
8. **The 1959 view:** published labeled approximate, before every later clearance is dated.

## Added by the owner the same day (11:43 ET)

9. **The island.** One image of only what was removed: the buildings, streets and ground of
   1952 inside the highway's boundary, drawn alone, like an island floating in space, with the
   boundary as its edge ("inside the membrane boundary or something").
10. **Keep the data visualizations.** The charts shown during the ground work stay in the
    story and its write-up: the plan of where the trench was filled (its coverage), the three
    street-level cross sections (today, rebuilt, the 1934 points), and the rest of the method
    charts.
