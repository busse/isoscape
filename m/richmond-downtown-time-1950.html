How this view was made

Downtown Richmond, from the Capitol to Tobacco Row, 1950. A frame 3 km across and 2 km deep (6.00 km²), centered on 37.5340°N, 77.4268°W.

The year 1950: Mid-century how time is drawn →

The last scene before the turnpike.

Measured: 1,867 buildings standing today that had been built by 1950. Their years: 1,228 from the city assessor’s year built, 608 from OpenStreetMap’s start_date, 31 curated. Anything demolished since is missing unless traced or curated below.

Traced: 729 buildings that no longer stand, traced from Sanborn fire-insurance maps and drawn in the maps’ own colors (pink brick, yellow frame, blue stone, gray iron). Each appears from the first map that shows it, though it may be older. When it went: 0 by a later map that no longer shows it, 43 by the year the building now on the site went up, 49 by an expressway through the site, and 637 assumed gone by 1960. Heights are guessed from the material: brick and stone three stories, frame two.

Streets: a street is drawn only where a building of the year stood within reach of it or the town plan laid it out: 1,617 of today’s are left off. Whatever the era rules do not date (parks, cemeteries, dams, piers, parking aisles) is drawn in today’s scene only.

Curated for this year, from published sources; positions are drawn from today’s street grid and published dimensions, not surveyed:

  • Free Bridge (the Ninth Street Bridge) (1873–1972). The free road bridge from the foot of 9th Street to Manchester, opened in June 1873 (RVA Mag; Dogtown Dish dates the span it describes to 1878, after the flood of 1877) and closed after the floods of 1969 and 1972, when the Manchester Bridge replaced it. Built "rather low over the river": its deck is drawn 8 m over the water, an assumption. Its line is today's Manchester Bridge's, which the USGS 1:24,000 Richmond quadrangle of 1934 shows it on ("9th St Bridge", just downstream of the Richmond and Petersburg bridge), from the corner of 9th Street to the foot of Commerce Road in Manchester: approximate. Drawn only in builds made with --with next: the 1925 and 1950 prints (version 4) were drawn before it. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
  • Richmond and Petersburg (Atlantic Coast Line) approach to Byrd Street Station (1838–1971). The track from the north end of the Richmond and Petersburg bridge (OSM w710689016) across the Haxall Canal into Byrd Street Station at Seventh and Byrd Streets, the R&P's original Richmond station and later the Atlantic Coast Line's headquarters, closed in 1958. Today nothing of it is mapped north of the bridge. Approximate: one track along the middle of the station's throat, read by eye from the USGS 1:24,000 Richmond quadrangles of 1934 and 1956 (which show the line crossing the canal and fanning into the station yard between Seventh and Eighth Streets south of Byrd) and joined to the bridge's mapped end, about 20 m east of where the maps put it. Drawn with the bridge, from 1838 to 1970 (the R&P's depot stood at 8th and Byrd before Byrd Street Station, and the 1886 Sanborn map, volume 1, sheet 10, shows its tracks fanning in between 7th and 9th south of Canal); the years are the bridge's, not the track's. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

The ground is today’s: cuts and fills since (the interstate, the floodwall) are in the elevation.

Camera how the camera works →

Looking north (bearing 351°), a fixed bearing with no snapping. Orthographic, tilted 30° down from the horizon: ground running into the picture is drawn at 0.5× its length and heights at 0.87×. 2 px per meter across the frame; the whole drawing is 6,048 × 2,727 px at 1:1.

Sources where the data comes from →

  • OpenStreetMap: 4,418 features read from the cached download, shared with region richmond-i195-rocketts.
  • USGS 3DEP elevation (United States (U.S. Geological Survey); bare-earth lidar, 1 m across most of the US, served by the National Map), fetched at 2 m per pixel and resampled to a 5 m grid. The ground runs from -0.1 m to 56.8 m above sea level (56.9 m of relief), drawn 2.5× taller than life. Buildings and bridges keep their true heights.

Ground what the ground is made of →

From the map, 33 mapped areas of water, islands, parks, woods and other cover, and 829 ribbons of road, path, rail, canal and weir. Painted in 200 strips from back to front, 10 m deep each, so hills hide what is behind them (why). Contours every 2 m with every 5th line heavier: 48,842 segments.

Rivers and rapids how rapids are read →

9,125 grid cells (about 22.8 ha) read as rapids: places on a river where its surface, recovered from the lidar, falls faster than 4 m per km along its channel, measured over 200 m. Rock standing more than 1 m clear of the water around it is drawn as boulders; lakes, basins and the sea get neither. Both are inferred from the lidar, not observed.

Buildings where heights come from →

1,867 buildings, extruded from footprints of at least 12 m² that lie wholly inside the frame, from OpenStreetMap.

  • 34 (2%) from a height tag
  • 300 (16%) from the number of floors, at 3.4 m a floor
  • 1,533 (82%) guessed from the building’s type and size

Bridges how decks are placed →

471 bridge spans of up to 22 m, so long bridges sort among the buildings. Deck heights are rules of thumb: Manchester 24 m, Mayo 6 m, Potterfield 3 m, and any other bridge 7 m per OSM layer.

Drawing paint order →

The paper palette, a dot on every visible corner, on a ground slab 18 m thick.
