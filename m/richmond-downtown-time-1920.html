How this view was made

Downtown Richmond, from the Capitol to Tobacco Row, 1920. A frame 3 km across and 2 km deep (6.00 km²), centered on 37.5340°N, 77.4268°W.

The year 1920: Two cities joined how time is drawn →

The new Mayo Bridge (1913) joins Richmond and Manchester, annexed in 1910.

Measured: 1,726 buildings standing today that had been built by 1920. Their years: 1,115 from the city assessor’s year built, 600 from OpenStreetMap’s start_date, 11 curated. 251 are new since 1910, roofed in gold. Anything demolished since is missing unless traced or curated below.

Traced: 922 buildings that no longer stand, traced from Sanborn fire-insurance maps and drawn in the maps’ own colors (pink brick, yellow frame, blue stone, gray iron). Each appears from the first map that shows it, though it may be older. When it went: 735 by a later map that no longer shows it, 27 by the year the building now on the site went up, 29 by an expressway through the site, and 131 assumed gone by 1960. Heights are guessed from the material: brick and stone three stories, frame two.

No curated features stand in this year; roads, rail and water follow the series’ era rules.

The ground is today’s: cuts and fills since (the interstate, the floodwall) are in the elevation.

Camera how the camera works →

Looking north (bearing 351°), a fixed bearing with no snapping. Orthographic, tilted 30° down from the horizon: ground running into the picture is drawn at 0.5× its length and heights at 0.87×. 2 px per meter across the frame; the whole drawing is 6,048 × 2,731 px at 1:1.

Sources where the data comes from →

  • OpenStreetMap: 9,317 features read from the cached download, shared with region richmond-i195-rocketts.
  • USGS 3DEP elevation (United States (U.S. Geological Survey); bare-earth lidar, 1 m across most of the US, served by the National Map), fetched at 2 m per pixel and resampled to a 5 m grid. The ground runs from -0.1 m to 57.2 m above sea level (57.3 m of relief), drawn 2.5× taller than life. Buildings and bridges keep their true heights.

Ground what the ground is made of →

From the map, 216 mapped areas of water, islands, parks, woods and other cover, and 1,561 ribbons of road, path, rail, canal and weir. Painted in 200 strips from back to front, 10 m deep each, so hills hide what is behind them (why). Contours every 2 m with every 5th line heavier: 49,756 segments.

Rivers and rapids how rapids are read →

4,553 grid cells (about 11.4 ha) read as rapids: places where the river’s surface, recovered from the lidar, falls faster than 3.5 m per 100 m, measured over 40 m. Rock standing more than 1 m clear of that surface is drawn as boulders. Both are inferred from the lidar, not observed.

Buildings where heights come from →

1,726 buildings, extruded from footprints of at least 12 m² that lie wholly inside the frame, from OpenStreetMap.

  • 30 (2%) from a height tag
  • 279 (16%) from the number of floors, at 3.4 m a floor
  • 1,417 (82%) guessed from the building’s type and size

Bridges how decks are placed →

496 bridge spans of up to 22 m, so long bridges sort among the buildings. Deck heights are rules of thumb: Manchester 24 m, Mayo 6 m, Potterfield 3 m, and any other bridge 7 m per OSM layer.

Drawing paint order →

The paper palette, a dot on every visible corner, on a ground slab 18 m thick.
