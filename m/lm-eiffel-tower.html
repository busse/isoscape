Eiffel Tower — Paris

Drawn by Chris Busse with isoscape, a free, open-source tool he built with Claude, from OpenStreetMap and public elevation data; how it's made. In the explorer you can pan and zoom the full drawing and compare it with other maps.

How this view was made

Eiffel Tower — Paris. A frame 1.5 km across and 1.95 km deep (2.92 km²), centered on 48.8583°N, 2.2945°E.

Camera how the camera works →

Looking north (bearing 358°), turned 1.6° counter-clockwise from the configured 0° so the street grid runs along the diagonals. Orthographic, tilted 30° down from the horizon: ground running into the picture is drawn at 0.5× its length and heights at 0.87×. 4 px per meter across the frame; the whole drawing is 6,048 × 4,345 px at 1:1.

Sources where the data comes from →

  • OpenStreetMap: 18,361 features read from the cached download.
  • IGN RGE ALTI elevation (France (Institut national de l’information géographique et forestière); bare-earth model at 1 m, from airborne lidar in cities, along coasts and in forests, and image correlation elsewhere), fetched at 2 m per pixel and resampled to a 5 m grid. The ground runs from 26.3 m to 69.5 m above sea level (43.2 m of relief), drawn 2× taller than life. Buildings and bridges keep their true heights.

Ground what the ground is made of →

From the map, 381 mapped areas of water, islands, parks, woods and other cover, and 4,242 ribbons of road, path, rail, canal and weir. Painted in 195 strips from back to front, 10 m deep each, so hills hide what is behind them (why). Contours every 2 m with every 5th line heavier: 17,287 segments.

Rivers and rapids how rapids are read →

No stretch of water read as rapids: places where the river’s surface, recovered from the lidar, falls faster than 0 m per 100 m, measured over 40 m. Rock standing more than 1 m clear of that surface is drawn as boulders. Both are inferred from the lidar, not observed.

Buildings where heights come from →

2,370 buildings, extruded from footprints of at least 12 m² that lie wholly inside the frame, from OpenStreetMap.

  • 215 (9%) from a height tag
  • 1,496 (63%) from the number of floors, at 3.4 m a floor
  • 659 (28%) guessed from the building’s type and size

Bridges how decks are placed →

369 bridge spans of up to 22 m, so long bridges sort among the buildings. Deck heights are rules of thumb: every bridge 7 m per OSM layer.

Drawing paint order →

The paper palette, a dot on every visible corner, on a ground slab 12 m thick.
