How this view was made

Broad and Belvidere, Richmond: the four corners, 1924. A frame 560 m across and 420 m deep (0.24 km²), centered on 37.5487°N, 77.4472°W.

The year 1924: Broad and Belvidere, Richmond, from the Sanborn fire-insurance map of 1924 how time is drawn →

Buildings traced from the Sanborn map of Richmond, volume 1 (1924), sheets 33 and 34, fitted to today's street corners (mean miss 2.3 m on sheet 33, 0.7 m on sheet 34). An auto row: sales rooms, repair shops and filling stations. Buildings standing today are drawn where the assessor dates them 1924 or earlier. Working draft.

Measured: 122 buildings standing today that had been built by 1924. Their years: 121 from the city assessor’s year built, 1 from OpenStreetMap’s start_date. Anything demolished since is missing unless traced or curated below.

Traced: 178 buildings that no longer stand, traced from Sanborn fire-insurance maps and drawn in the maps’ own colors (pink brick, yellow frame, blue stone, gray iron). Each appears from the first map that shows it, though it may be older. When it went: 101 by a later map that no longer shows it, 40 by the year the building now on the site went up, 0 by an expressway through the site, and 37 assumed gone by 1960. Heights are guessed from the material: brick and stone three stories, frame two.

Streets: a street is drawn only where a building of the year stood within reach of it or the town plan laid it out: 11 of today’s are left off. Whatever the era rules do not date (parks, cemeteries, dams, piers, parking aisles) is drawn in today’s scene only.

Curated for this year, from published sources; positions are drawn from today’s street grid and published dimensions, not surveyed:

  • Richmond and Petersburg (Atlantic Coast Line) approach to Byrd Street Station (1838–1971). The track from the north end of the Richmond and Petersburg bridge (OSM w710689016) across the Haxall Canal into Byrd Street Station at Seventh and Byrd Streets, the R&P's original Richmond station and later the Atlantic Coast Line's headquarters, closed in 1958. Today nothing of it is mapped north of the bridge. Approximate: one track along the middle of the station's throat, read by eye from the USGS 1:24,000 Richmond quadrangles of 1934 and 1956 (which show the line crossing the canal and fanning into the station yard between Seventh and Eighth Streets south of Byrd) and joined to the bridge's mapped end, about 20 m east of where the maps put it. Drawn with the bridge, from 1838 to 1970 (the R&P's depot stood at 8th and Byrd before Byrd Street Station, and the 1886 Sanborn map, volume 1, sheet 10, shows its tracks fanning in between 7th and 9th south of Canal); the years are the bridge's, not the track's. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
  • Chesapeake and Ohio approach to the Church Hill Tunnel (1873–1926). The C&O's line to the Church Hill Tunnel's west portal, by 18th Street just north of Marshall: it left the old Virginia Central line west of 17th Street and curved southeast. The tunnel opened in 1873, collapsed on October 2, 1925, and its western end was sealed the following spring, so it is drawn through 1925 and not after. Its course: OSM's abandoned branch (w169784705) from the junction with today's Peninsula Subdivision south to 17th Street; then the C&O spur shown on the 1924–25 Sanborn map, volume 1, sheet 24, crossing the block between 17th and 18th Streets, Marshall and Clay, between the Richmond Cold Storage and International Harvester warehouses (the sheet fitted to the four corners of the block, to about a meter, and the line set midway between the warehouses standing today); then straight across 18th Street to the portal mapped in OSM (the tunnel, w6036486). Approximate. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

The ground is today’s: cuts and fills since (the interstate, the floodwall) are in the elevation.

Camera how the camera works →

Looking northeast (bearing 37°), a fixed bearing with no snapping. Orthographic, tilted 30° down from the horizon: ground running into the picture is drawn at 0.5× its length and heights at 0.87×. 4 px per meter across the frame; the whole drawing is 2,288 × 1,093 px at 1:1.

Sources where the data comes from →

  • OpenStreetMap: 599 features read from the cached download.
  • USGS 3DEP elevation (United States (U.S. Geological Survey); bare-earth lidar, 1 m across most of the US, served by the National Map), fetched at 5 m per pixel and resampled to a 5 m grid. The ground runs from 48.8 m to 58.7 m above sea level (9.9 m of relief), drawn 2.5× taller than life. Buildings and bridges keep their true heights.

Ground what the ground is made of →

From the map, 0 mapped areas of water, islands, parks, woods and other cover, and 134 ribbons of road, path, rail, canal and weir. Painted in 42 strips from back to front, 10 m deep each, so hills hide what is behind them (why). Contours every 1 m with every 5th line heavier: 1,061 segments.

Rivers and rapids how rapids are read →

No stretch of water read as rapids: places on a river where its surface, recovered from the lidar, falls faster than 4 m per km along its channel, measured over 200 m. Rock standing more than 1 m clear of the water around it is drawn as boulders; lakes, basins and the sea get neither. Both are inferred from the lidar, not observed.
