How this view was made

Broad Street Station, 1943, 1943. A frame 1 km across and 800 m deep (0.80 km²), centered on 37.5607°N, 77.4663°W.

The year 1943: Broad Street Station at its height how time is drawn →

The wartime peak: 57 trains a day, a record 33,324 passengers on April 22, 1943.

Measured: 390 buildings standing today that had been built by 1943. Their years: 389 from the city assessor’s year built, 1 from OpenStreetMap’s start_date. Anything demolished since is missing unless traced or curated below.

Traced: 64 buildings that no longer stand, traced from Sanborn fire-insurance maps and drawn in the maps’ own colors (pink brick, yellow frame, blue stone, gray iron). Each appears from the first map that shows it, though it may be older. When it went: 0 by a later map that no longer shows it, 0 by the year the building now on the site went up, 0 by an expressway through the site, and 64 assumed gone by 1960. Heights are guessed from the material: brick and stone three stories, frame two.

Streetcars: 13 car lines in red, each placed on today’s streets from the corners its sources name (listed below), with 1 car along them. Small streets are drawn only where a house of the year stood nearby: 95 laid out since are left off.

Modeled close up: 27 parts of one building, each from its plans and photographs, in the building’s own colors; 104 pieces of platform canopy; and 68 cars of standing trains, their number and makeup illustrative. Heights are approximate. how the station is modeled →

  • Westhampton line (1901–1947). West on Floyd Avenue from Robinson Street to the belt line railroad, under it in a tunnel, then a little north to Grove Avenue; out Grove to Three Chopt Road; then on its own right-of-way beside Three Chopt to Towana Road, and on (the line of today's Campus Drive) to Westhampton Park, which became the University of Richmond's campus in 1914. The right-of-way beside Three Chopt is drawn along the road itself. [1] [2]
  • Belmont line (1895–1949). The Broad Street line's extension west from Robinson Street to Sheppard, south to Cary, later carried on eleven blocks to Garrett Street. Which street carried the last stretch is read from the 1925 plate's 'Belmont' label. [1] [2]
  • Broad Street line (1888–1949). Broad Street, the spine of the system, from the Belmont line's turn at Sheppard Street east to Church Hill. The east end is approximate. [1] [2]
  • Main Street line (1888–1949). West Main Street, which the line turned into a commercial street through the Fan, east to Shockoe. The west end is approximate. [1] [2]
  • Harrison Street line (1890–1949). A crosstown link on Harrison Street between the Main, Floyd and Broad lines, as the 1925 plate shows it. [1]
  • Reservoir (Byrd) Park line (1895–1949). Down Robinson Street to Reservoir Park (Byrd Park), one of the trolley's favorite destinations, along Lakeview Avenue as the 1925 plate labels it. The far end is approximate. [1] [2] [3]
  • Clay Street line (1888–1949). Sprague's first line of 1888 ran from Church Hill to Clay and Hancock Streets; the 1925 plate still shows Clay Street. The ends are approximate. [1] [2]
  • Brookland and Lakeside line (1891–1949). Over the First Street viaduct (1891) into Barton Heights, then west along Brookland Park Boulevard, north up the median of Chamberlayne Avenue, west on Laburnum and north on Hermitage Road to Lakeside Park and Lewis Ginter's Lakeside Wheel Club. The run beyond Hermitage Road along Lakeside Avenue is approximate. [1] [2] [3]
  • Highland Park line (1893–1949). North on Fifth Street over the Northside Viaduct (1892) to Chestnut Hill and Highland Park, and after 1908 round a loop by Meadowbridge Road, Milton Street and Highland Street (the cross street joining them, Second Avenue here, is approximate). The Hull Street–Highland Park line was Richmond's last: the last car ran on 25 November 1949. [1] [2] [3]
  • Marshall Street line (Fulton) (1911–1949). Across Shockoe Valley on the Marshall Street Viaduct (1911, since demolished: drawn straight) from College Street to 21st Street at Jefferson Park, out Marshall Street, and down to Fulton along Williamsburg Avenue. The route from Marshall Street down to Fulton is approximate. [1] [2]
  • Venable Street line (1888–1949). North on 17th Street, east along Venable Street through Union Hill, and up 25th Street to Nine Mile Road, the road the Seven Pines line took out of the city (chartered along 25th to 29th Streets and Nine Mile Road). The federal government bought the Seven Pines line in 1918 for a wartime plant; how far cars ran out Nine Mile Road in 1925 is not certain. [1] [2] [3]
  • Jefferson Avenue line (1890–1949). Up 21st Street and out Jefferson Avenue past Jefferson Park, one of the trolley's pleasure grounds, as the 1925 plate shows it. [1] [2]
  • Hull Street line (1888–1949). Over the Mayo Bridge into Manchester and out Hull Street. Its last car, No. 408 on the Hull Street–Highland Park run, ended Richmond's streetcars on 25 November 1949. The outer end is approximate. [1] [2]

Camera how the camera works →

Looking northeast (bearing 45°), a fixed bearing with no snapping. Orthographic, tilted 38° down from the horizon: ground running into the picture is drawn at 0.62× its length and heights at 0.78×. 3.5 px per meter across the frame; the whole drawing is 3,548 × 2,042 px at 1:1.

Rivers and rapids how rapids are read →

No stretch of water read as rapids: places where the river’s surface, recovered from the lidar, falls faster than 3.5 m per 100 m, measured over 40 m. Rock standing more than 1 m clear of that surface is drawn as boulders. Both are inferred from the lidar, not observed.
