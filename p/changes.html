Changes

The maps here are drawn from data and from the historic sources cited for each one. As the tools and the research get better, a published map is sometimes corrected and redrawn. This page lists those changes, newest first. A print ordered after a change is made from the new version; the version is part of each print file's name (-v2, -v3).

How versions work from here

November 18, 2026. From today, every map and print goes through the same stages before it is sold. A rough draft tests whether a subject works. A working draft is composed at full size, with its notes written and sourced. A final draft is a release candidate, checked once against a fixed list. Final production is the print for sale. A print's title block now gets a version number only at final production, and that number names an edition someone could own. Drafts are never sold and never listed here. If a print for sale has a fault and nobody has ordered it yet, it may be corrected in place without a new version number. This page will still say what changed and when. Once anyone has ordered an edition, it stays as it is, and any change becomes a new edition. Numbers already printed stay as they are. The production board shows where each piece stands. November 18, 2026 (Byrd Park Pump House cutaway)

New: The New Pump-House, Richmond, about 1885: Cutaway (plate BPPH·01, version 1). Richmond's pump house on the canal at Byrd Park cut open to its machine room, where canal water turned three turbine wheels and the wheels pumped drinking water up to the New Reservoir. Below it, two flat sections follow the water through a wheel and through a pump, and a small map shows where the water went. It opens a new set in Buildings in their time, Byrd Park Pump House, 1885. November 18, 2026 (Main Street Station corrected)

Broad Street Station, 1943: Main Street Station redrawn at its measured height. Nobody had ordered these editions, so they are corrected in place as 3.1, and each title block still says 3:

  • Main Street Station (BSS·07) 3.1: the station redrawn, and the note corrected.
  • The Railroad Map (BSS·M) 3.1: the station redrawn.
  • The framed set, The Railroads and Fourteen Places (BSS·00) 3.1: the station redrawn on its map and panel 7, and note 7 corrected.
  • The Triple Crossing (BSS·08) 3.1: the station redrawn where it stands at the top edge.

Main Street Station is now drawn at the height measured on three Historic American Buildings Survey photographs of 1971 (VA-848-10, -11 and -12), scaled by the front's own width of 102 feet: the ridge about 115 feet over Main Street, and the clock tower about 148 feet to the finial (all approximate). It had been drawn about a fifth too low, from the 110 feet The Railroad Gazette gave in 1900, before the station was built. Stop 7's note now gives the tower as about 148 feet to the finial (it said 110 feet). No other print changes. The correction was made with Claude, in code; this entry was drafted with Claude.

November 18, 2026

Broad Street Station, 1943: the set is complete. The set's map, The Railroad Map (plate BSS·M), and the framed set, The Railroads and Fourteen Places (BSS·00), are redrawn as version 3, and box 3 is now Scott's Addition (BSS·16, version 1). They were redrawn with Claude, in code, and checked against the set's inspection list at full print size.

  • On the map and the framed set, the numbered badges are set off the stations with leaders, and the labels sit beside their subjects. Both carry the corrections listed below for the fourteen places.
  • Box 3 is now Scott's Addition, the warehouse district between the RF&P and the Belt Line: a new print, plate BSS·16, version 1. It replaces Hermitage Road (BSS·03), which is withdrawn: its picture could not carry the place. Its plate number is not used again.
  • One known fault, stated plainly: Main Street Station is drawn about a fifth too low, on the map and on stop 7, and stop 7's note gives 110 feet to the finial where photographs measure about 148 feet. Both will be corrected.

The title block on each print gives its version. This entry was drafted with Claude.

November 17, 2026 (Richmond's Streetcars, the last six)

Richmond's Streetcars, 1925: the set's map, the framed set and stops 1, 7, 8 and 9 redrawn. The framed set, The Lines and Fourteen Stops (plate STC·00), and the map alone, The System Map (STC·M), are now version 4; Westhampton Park (STC·01), Church Hill and Jefferson Park (STC·07), Seventh and Broad (STC·08) and Manchester (STC·09) are version 3. They were redrawn with Claude, in code, and inspected tile by tile at full print size. They carry the corrections listed below for the other ten stops (car lines down the middle of divided streets, bridges at the line's width, Chamberlayne Avenue at grade before 1992, the Fair Grounds spur's low deck, woods filled, streams mapped off their beds left out), and these besides:

  • Bridges and viaducts that carry a car line follow the line, so the line no longer steps sideways onto and off a deck.
  • The Westhampton line runs on its own strip beside Three Chopt Road rather than down the middle of the road (approximate).
  • The Fulton line on Williamsburg Avenue is moved to clear the Armitage building, which it ran through (approximate).
  • The Marshall Street Viaduct's piers are respaced, and its ends stand on abutments rather than hanging out from the bluff (approximate).
  • Manchester's note, on its print and on the framed set's card 9, says the Mayo Bridge is drawn as a plain deck.

With these, every print of the set is redrawn and the whole set, the map, the framed set and all fourteen stops, is for sale. The title block on each print gives its version. This entry was drafted with Claude.

November 17, 2026 (evening)

Richmond, 1925 (plate RVA·01) and Richmond, 1950 (RVA·04), version 5: redrawn. Both prints were redrawn with Claude, in code, with the corrections made for Richmond, 1886 (isoscape build --with next,next2), and inspected again, tile by tile, at full print size.

  • Version 5 adds the Ninth Street Bridge, the Free Bridge, which stood from 1873 to 1972 and was missing. It crosses the river from the foot of 9th Street, at the left edge of the print. Its line and its height are approximate: it is drawn on the line of today's Manchester Bridge, which replaced it, about 8 meters over the water.

November 17, 2026 (later)

Richmond, 1886 (plate RVA·03), Richmond, 1737–2026 (RVA·06) and Richmond, Then & Now (RVA·07), version 3: redrawn. The three prints were taken off sale on November 16 while they were inspected again, tile by tile, at full print size. These faults in version 2 are corrected:

  • The James River and Kanawha Canal and its Great Basin, gone today, were drawn on today's ground: the basin's near bank came out as a sawtooth cliff and the canal as broken blue shards. They are now still water at their own level, with clean banks. In 1886 the basin is what the Sanborn map of that year shows left of it (between 9th and 10th Streets, and a narrow slip between 11th and 12th), with the canal running in from Tredegar.
  • No bridge crossed the river to Manchester in 1886. Mayo's Bridge, the Free Bridge at 9th Street (1873 to 1972), the Richmond and Petersburg railroad bridge and the Richmond and Danville's (today the Southern's) are drawn in the years each stood; in the 1865 panel none is, since all were burned in April 1865. The older bridges are drawn on their successors' lines and heights, an approximation.
  • The Seaboard's line through Shockoe Valley (it reached Richmond in 1900) and today's station platforms were drawn in earlier years. They are left out before 1900 and 1901.
  • Shockoe Creek, still open on the 1886 Sanborn map, is drawn open in 1886.
  • In the 1970 and 1990 panels of Richmond, 1737–2026 twenty-two towers are dated from published sources (Wikipedia's list of Richmond's tallest buildings, its James Center article) instead of the assessor's records, and three tall buildings with no source are left to 2026. The captions say what the early panels can show: before 1886, only the buildings that survive, the curated landmarks and the streets.
  • Each print says that the ground is today's and that traced buildings' heights are assumed from their material, legibly on the smallest print sold.

What is approximate: the Free Bridge's line and its 8 m deck, the canal's course west of 6th Street, the years the basin was cut back (about 1880) and gone (by 1895), and the dates of the bridges where the history file says so. Richmond, 1865 (RVA·02) stays off sale: with no fire-insurance map before 1886 it shows only the buildings that survive and a few landmarks, which reads as an empty city. The 1925 and 1950 prints (version 4) do not yet show the Free Bridge; they will in their next version. The title block on each redrawn print gives version 3. This entry was drafted with Claude.

November 17, 2026

Richmond, 1925 (plate RVA·01) and Richmond, 1950 (RVA·04), version 4: redrawn. Both prints were inspected again tile by tile after version 3 went on sale, and these faults in version 3 are corrected:

  • In 1925 the Mayo Bridge's south span, from Mayo's Island to Manchester, was missing. It is drawn whole.
  • A tower finished in 1965, the Ross Building at 8th and Main Streets, stood in both years (the assessor dates it 1925). It is gone, and its lot is left empty.
  • Traced Sanborn buildings stood in the river, on the canal, under the rail viaducts and in later embankments (the interstate's). They are left out.
  • The Richmond and Petersburg railroad bridge ended in the air at the canal bank. It now lands on its approach to Byrd Street Station, and in 1925 the C&O runs to the Church Hill Tunnel (it collapsed that October); both approaches are placed approximately, from the USGS maps of 1934 and 1956 and the 1924–25 Sanborn map.
  • Modern roundabouts, the Canal Walk's canal bridges, parking-lot drives and planting beds were shown. They are left out.

The years that date later works (roundabouts from 1990, drives and parking from 1960, canal bridges from 1999) are approximate, and every traced building's height is assumed from its material, as the print says. The title block gives version 4.

Richmond, 1925 (plate RVA·01), Richmond, 1950 (RVA·04), Richmond, 2026 (RVA·05) and Richmond and the James (RVA·08), version 3: redrawn. The four views of downtown Richmond were inspected tile by tile, corrected in code, and drawn again from the same data.

  • The same corrections as The James, below: the elevation is read in place, the hill shading is smooth, rapids and rocks are drawn only where the river falls, shorelines follow the mapped banks, and bridges and ramps end properly.
  • The Capitol stands at its height: Jefferson's central block at 26 m (85 ft to the roof), with the wings of 1904–06 from 1906 (their 18 m is approximate). It had been a low 7 m block.
  • St. John's Church, a one-story frame church, is drawn about 9 m tall (approximate) instead of at the 16 m guess for a church.
  • Eleven buildings the city assessor misdated now stand only from their real years: the Hotel John Marshall (1929) is gone from 1925, and apartment buildings put up since 2005, which had appeared in the 1925 and 1950 views, are gone from both.
  • In 1925 and 1950, buildings traced from Sanborn fire-insurance maps that would stand in today's river, or under the rail viaducts of 1901, are left out: the maps show buildings, not the old shorelines.
  • In 1950, streets that now bridge roads built later (the expressway, I-95) run at grade, and surface parking lots (dated from about 1960) are left out.
  • In 2026, two apartment buildings that OpenStreetMap tags far too tall are drawn by their floors: The Hues at City View (about 22 m, not 80 m) and The Locks at 321 (48 m, not 96 m). A building over the Haxall Canal stands on its banks instead of sinking below the ground in front of it.
  • Each print's credit line names exactly the sources its map was drawn from, set large enough to read at the smallest size the print is sold in (at least 7 pt on an 8 × 12 in print). The 1925 and 1950 prints add that the ground is today's, from lidar, so cuts and fills made since (the interstate, the floodwall) show in it.

The plates are unchanged; the title block on each print now gives version 3.

The James, I-195 to Rocketts Landing (plate RVA·09) and Gwynn's Island (plate GWY·01), version 3: redrawn. Both maps were inspected tile by tile, corrected in code, and drawn again from the same data.

  • The elevation data had been read out of place, so the ground did not sit exactly under the mapped streets and shores. It is now read in place.
  • The hill shading is smooth: steep banks no longer show dark smears or stepped edges.
  • On the James, rapids and rocks are drawn only where the river falls, among the islands above the Mayo Bridge and at the dam by Belle Isle, and no longer on the dry bed south of Belle Isle or in a wastewater basin by Rocketts.
  • Shorelines follow the mapped banks, without the saw-tooth edges they had along the tidal reach at Rocketts and on the bluffs and spit of Gwynn's Island.
  • Bridges and ramps end properly, on the ground they reach, with no short steep stubs.
  • On Gwynn's Island, the edges of woods and fields are smooth, no longer stepped by the 30 m land-cover grid.
  • Each print's credit line names exactly the sources its map was drawn from. Gwynn's Island had credited Sanborn fire-insurance maps it does not use; it now credits OpenStreetMap, USGS 3DEP elevation, the USGS National Land Cover Database and Microsoft's building footprints.

The plates are unchanged; the title block on each print now gives version 3.

November 15, 2026

Every print now carries its plate. Each design has a permanent catalog number, its plate (RVA·01 is Richmond, 1925; STC·02 is stop 2 of Richmond's Streetcars; LMA·25 is Alcatraz). A small title block in the corner of every print and canvas gives the plate with the piece's place in the catalog (collection, set and piece), its version, the year it was drawn, the point it was drawn around and the direction it faces. On the two set sheets the block sits in the header. The art is unchanged.
