Changes

The maps here are drawn from data and from the historic sources cited for each one. As the tools and the research get better, a published map is sometimes corrected and redrawn. This page lists those changes, newest first. A print ordered after a change is made from the new version; the version is part of each print file's name (-v2, -v3).

November 16, 2026

The James, I-195 to Rocketts Landing (plate RVA·09) and Gwynn's Island (plate GWY·01), version 3: redrawn. Both maps were inspected tile by tile, corrected in code, and drawn again from the same data.

  • The elevation data had been read out of place, so the ground did not sit exactly under the mapped streets and shores. It is now read in place.
  • The hill shading is smooth: steep banks no longer show dark smears or stepped edges.
  • On the James, rapids and rocks are drawn only where the river falls, among the islands above the Mayo Bridge and at the dam by Belle Isle, and no longer on the dry bed south of Belle Isle or in a wastewater basin by Rocketts.
  • Shorelines follow the mapped banks, without the saw-tooth edges they had along the tidal reach at Rocketts and on the bluffs and spit of Gwynn's Island.
  • Bridges and ramps end properly, on the ground they reach, with no short steep stubs.
  • On Gwynn's Island, the edges of woods and fields are smooth, no longer stepped by the 30 m land-cover grid.
  • Each print's credit line names exactly the sources its map was drawn from. Gwynn's Island had credited Sanborn fire-insurance maps it does not use; it now credits OpenStreetMap, USGS 3DEP elevation, the USGS National Land Cover Database and Microsoft's building footprints.

The plates are unchanged; the title block on each print now gives version 3.

Drafted with Claude; see the artist statement.

New: Broad Street Station, 1943: Cutaway (plate BSS·15, version 1). The station cut down the middle, from the Broad Street doors to the trains, with the same cut seen flat below it and a traveler's way from the street to the train. It is the first building cutaway. Every part is marked by how sure it is: drawn from a plan or section, from written accounts, or approximate. The cutaway, its callouts and its sources

November 15, 2026

Every print now carries its plate. Each design has a permanent catalog number, its plate (RVA·01 is Richmond, 1925; STC·02 is stop 2 of Richmond's Streetcars; LMA·25 is Alcatraz). A small title block in the corner of every print and canvas gives the plate with the piece's place in the catalog (collection, set and piece), its version, the year it was drawn, the point it was drawn around and the direction it faces. On the two set sheets the block sits in the header. The art is unchanged. Every design is now version 2, except the menu, the system map and stop 2 of Richmond's Streetcars, 1925, which are version 3.

New: Broad Street Station, 1943. Richmond's union station at its wartime height and the railroads that ran from it: the railroad map framed by fourteen places, the map alone, and a print for each place. The first of a new kind of map, one building drawn close up in its own time. The places and their sources

Richmond's Streetcars, 1925, version 2: the menu, the system map, and stop 2 (State Fair Grounds).

  • The fair grounds were drawn as a rectangle square to north with a small oval in it, which matched neither the streets around them nor the sources. They are now traced from the 1925 Sanborn fire-insurance sheet of the grounds: the triangle between the Boulevard and Hermitage Road, the fair's one-mile track (the note had said half-mile), and thirty-six of its buildings.
  • Today's buildings, streets and lots on the site (The Diamond among them) are no longer drawn there in the years the fair stood.

The other thirteen stop prints are unchanged (version 1).
