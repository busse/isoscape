About, credits and licenses

isoscape makes isometric dot-and-line views of real places from open map data. It is a sibling of dotscene. The two share a visual language, but dotscene is for drawing a scene by hand, and isoscape surveys one: you choose a frame on the world, and the data decides the rest. How it's made explains each step.

How it was made

isoscape was built, and the holiday shop is run, in chats with Claude, Anthropic's AI, using Claude Opus 5.5, directed and reviewed by Chris Busse. Claude wrote the code, traced the historic buildings, laid out the prints, and set up the shop. Chris Busse draws the maps with isoscape, in code, from open data; nothing is drawn by hand. The historic years are machine-traced sketches.

Try it yourself

Open a chat with Claude (we used Claude Opus 5.5) and say:

Use the GitHub repo busse/isoscape to make an isometric map of your place.

It takes a few minutes. Terrain works anywhere: from USGS lidar in the US, from national mapping agencies in France, Switzerland and Japan, and from the European Space Agency's 30 m Copernicus model everywhere else. Landmarks around the world shows one of each.

Inspiration

isoscape began as an attempt to draw Richmond the way Isometric SF draws San Francisco: Justin Chavez's isometric pixel-art map of the city. His write-up, how it was made, set out the idea this project follows: an isometric city view drawn from open map data. isoscape's code and drawing style are its own, but the idea came from his work.

Data

OpenStreetMap. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License (ODbL). The cached data/*/osm.json files are OSM data and carry that license. A rendered picture is a produced work. You may use it, but it must credit OpenStreetMap wherever it is shown. Every SVG isoscape writes carries the credit in its description, and every page shows it.

Historic scenes are sketches. Their lost buildings are traced by machine from Sanborn fire-insurance maps, so outlines and heights are approximate. They show the city's shape, not a building-by-building survey.

Elevation. In the US, from the USGS 3D Elevation Program (3DEP), served by the National Map. It is in the public domain. The USGS asks for, but does not require, a credit. data/*/dem.bin.gz is a resampled copy.

isoscape sources prints the same list. Every map names its own source in its caption.

Building footprints (some regions). Microsoft Global ML Building Footprints, under the ODbL like OSM. A view that uses them should credit Microsoft as well.

Land cover (some regions). The National Land Cover Database (NLCD 2021) from the USGS and the Multi-Resolution Land Characteristics Consortium. It is in the public domain.

Fire-insurance maps (dated series). Sanborn Fire Insurance Maps of Richmond, as digitized by the Library of Congress. The editions used were published long enough ago to be out of US copyright. Check each item's rights statement on the Library's site before reusing the scans. The traced footprints in history/sanborn/ are derived from them, and are free to reuse under CC0: see Data for those and the other datasets made for these maps, with their licenses.

Years built (dated series). The Richmond series uses the City of Richmond's Year of Construction layer, published on the city's ArcGIS open data service. Check the city's terms before redistributing data/*/yearbuilt.json. The curated history in history/ cites its sources item by item.

Sharing a picture

Put this line with the picture. Drop the second part for a view drawn without terrain, and outside the US use that source's credit from the table above instead of USGS 3DEP:

Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL) · Elevation: USGS 3DEP

and, where a region draws on them, add: · Building footprints: Microsoft (ODbL) · Land cover: NLCD 2021

Say what is estimated as well as what is measured. Many building heights are guesses, deck heights are rules of thumb, and rapids are inferred from the lidar (see Measured, inferred, guessed). A view used in planning or teaching should not be read as a survey.

Code

isoscape is written in TypeScript and runs directly on Node 22.18 or newer, with no runtime dependencies and no build step. package.json declares the MIT license. CLAUDE.md sets out the conventions for changing the code, and the README covers commands, region configs and adding a page like this one.
