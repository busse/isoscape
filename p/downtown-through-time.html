Downtown Richmond, VA, through time: the same blocks from 1737 to 2026

This is one view of downtown Richmond, Virginia, from the Capitol east to Tobacco Row with the James River in front, drawn as it stood in 26 years between 1737 and 2026. What changes, and when

Each link sets the view to the years it names.

  • 1737 and 2026: then and now. The town plan beside today's city.
  • 1737 and 1788: the town plan. William Mayo laid out the town for William Byrd II in 1737, a grid of two-acre squares east of Shockoe Creek, and the 1737 scene draws only the streets inside it (SAH Archipedia, Encyclopedia Virginia).
  • 1788 and 1800: the Great Basin. The canal's basin covered three blocks between Cary and Canal Streets, from 8th to 12th Street, and was finished about 1800 (HMDB, City of Richmond).
  • 1860 and 1865: the Evacuation Fire. Some 35 blocks burned as the Confederate government left the city in April 1865. The scene draws the burned district simplified, from 4th to 16th Street and down to the river (HMDB, The Cultural Landscape Foundation). Gallego Mills, by 1860 the largest building in Richmond, burned with it (HMDB).
  • 1870 and 1886: the basin filled. Railroad yards covered about half of the Great Basin in the 1880s and all of it by the 1920s. The 1886 scene draws what was left of it as the 1886 Sanborn fire-insurance map shows it (HMDB, Library of Congress).
  • 1886 and 1895: Shockoe Creek. A culvert begun in 1877 enclosed part of the creek, but the 1886 Sanborn map still shows it open from Broad Street to the Dock, so it is drawn until about 1890 (SAH Archipedia, Library of Congress).
  • 1950 and 1960: the turnpike. The Richmond-Petersburg Turnpike opened on 1 July 1958 and was designated I-95 a month later (VCU Libraries).
  • 1970 and 1980: the Downtown Expressway. It opened on 3 February 1976 (AARoads).
  • 1990 and 2000: the riverfront. The floodwall was dedicated on 21 October 1994 (HMDB), and the canal's downtown stretch was re-watered for the Canal Walk in 1999 (City of Richmond).

How it was made

Each scene draws the buildings standing today that had been built by its year, dated by OpenStreetMap or the city assessor's year built, plus a short list of things since lost, each with its sources. Buildings now gone come from the Sanborn fire-insurance maps of 1886, 1895, 1905, 1924–25 and 1950–52; they are sketches, and their outlines and heights are approximate. The ground in every year is today's. The method and its limits are in How it's made.
