# Changes

The maps here are drawn from data and from the historic sources cited for each one. As the
tools and the research get better, a published map is sometimes corrected and redrawn. This
page lists those changes, newest first. A print ordered after a change is made from the new
version; the version is part of each print file's name (`-v2`, `-v3`).

## November 15, 2026

**Every print now carries its plate.** Each design has a permanent catalog number, its plate
(RVA·01 is Richmond, 1925; STC·02 is stop 2 of Richmond's Streetcars; LMA·25 is Alcatraz). A
small title block in the corner of every print and canvas gives the plate with the piece's place
in the catalog (collection, set and piece), its version, the year it was drawn, the point it was
drawn around and the direction it faces. On the two set sheets the block sits in the header. The
art is unchanged. Every design is now version 2, except the menu, the system map and stop 2 of
Richmond's Streetcars, 1925, which are version 3.

**New: Broad Street Station, 1943.** Richmond's union station at its wartime height and the
railroads that ran from it: the railroad map framed by fourteen places, the map alone, and a
print for each place. The first of a new kind of map, one building drawn close up in its own
time. [The places and their sources](broad-street.md)

**Richmond's Streetcars, 1925, version 2: the menu, the system map, and stop 2 (State Fair
Grounds).**

- The fair grounds were drawn as a rectangle square to north with a small oval in it, which
  matched neither the streets around them nor the sources. They are now traced from the 1925
  Sanborn fire-insurance sheet of the grounds: the triangle between the Boulevard and
  Hermitage Road, the fair's one-mile track (the note had said half-mile), and thirty-six of
  its buildings.
- Today's buildings, streets and lots on the site (The Diamond among them) are no longer
  drawn there in the years the fair stood.

The other thirteen stop prints are unchanged (version 1).
