# Changes

The maps here are drawn from data and from the historic sources cited for each one. As the
tools and the research get better, a published map is sometimes corrected and redrawn. This
page lists those changes, newest first. A print ordered after a change is made from the new
version; the version is part of each print file's name (`-v2`, `-v3`).

## November 17, 2026 (night)

**Richmond's Streetcars, 1925: ten of the stop prints redrawn.** State Fair Grounds (plate
STC·02) is now version 4; Ginter Park (STC·03), Barton Heights (STC·04), Highland Park (STC·05),
Nine Mile Road (STC·06), Woodland Heights (STC·10), Forest Hill Park (STC·11), Byrd Park (STC·12),
The Fan (STC·13) and Broad and Laurel (STC·14) are version 3. They were redrawn with Claude, in
code, and inspected tile by tile at full print size. These faults are corrected:

- On divided streets a car line followed one side of the street, or jogged from one piece of the
  street to the next. It now runs down the middle of the street.
- Bridges and viaducts that carry a car line were narrower than the line. They are drawn at the
  line's width, with the line on them whole.
- Chamberlayne Avenue's bridges over the railroad north of Leigh Street were built in 1992. What
  crossed there in 1925 is not known here, so before 1992 the avenue is drawn crossing at grade
  (approximate).
- The Fair Grounds spur crosses the RF&P on a plain low deck, beside the Boulevard (approximate).
- Roads built later left their clearings in the woods of 1925. The woods are filled.
- Two streams mapped off their beds (one of them mapped where it runs underground) are left out.
- The notes on box 2 and box 4 are reworded: box 2 names the State Fair Grounds spur, and box 4
  no longer speaks of the creek, only of its valley.

The set's map, the framed set and the prints of stops 1, 7, 8 and 9 follow when their
corrections are done; until then they are not for sale. The title block on each print gives its
version. This entry was drafted with Claude.

## November 17, 2026 (evening)

**Richmond, 1925 (plate RVA·01) and Richmond, 1950 (RVA·04), version 5: redrawn.** Both prints
were redrawn with Claude, in code, with the corrections made for Richmond, 1886 (`isoscape build
--with next,next2`), and inspected again, tile by tile, at full print size.

- Version 5 adds the Ninth Street Bridge, the Free Bridge, which stood from 1873 to 1972 and was
  missing. It crosses the river from the foot of 9th Street, at the left edge of the print. Its
  line and its height are approximate: it is drawn on the line of today's Manchester Bridge, which
  replaced it, about 8 meters over the water.
- A frame building traced from the 1924–25 Sanborn map stood on the bridge's line by the canal
  bank, and the bridge's deck ran over its roof. It is left out, as buildings under the rail
  viaducts already were.
- Patches of woods along what became the interstate and the Downtown Expressway were drawn. They
  were planted with those roads, so they are left out.

The title block on each print gives version 5.

## November 17, 2026 (later)

**Richmond, 1886 (plate RVA·03), Richmond, 1737–2026 (RVA·06) and Richmond, Then & Now (RVA·07),
version 3: redrawn.** The three prints were taken off sale on November 16 while they were inspected
again, tile by tile, at full print size. These faults in version 2 are corrected:

- The James River and Kanawha Canal and its Great Basin, gone today, were drawn on today's ground:
  the basin's near bank came out as a sawtooth cliff and the canal as broken blue shards. They are
  now still water at their own level, with clean banks. In 1886 the basin is what the Sanborn map
  of that year shows left of it (between 9th and 10th Streets, and a narrow slip between 11th and
  12th), with the canal running in from Tredegar.
- No bridge crossed the river to Manchester in 1886. Mayo's Bridge, the Free Bridge at 9th Street
  (1873 to 1972), the Richmond and Petersburg railroad bridge and the Richmond and Danville's (today
  the Southern's) are drawn in the years each stood; in the 1865 panel none is, since all were
  burned in April 1865. The older bridges are drawn on their successors' lines and heights, an
  approximation.
- The Seaboard's line through Shockoe Valley (it reached Richmond in 1900) and today's station
  platforms were drawn in earlier years. They are left out before 1900 and 1901.
- Shockoe Creek, still open on the 1886 Sanborn map, is drawn open in 1886.
- In the 1970 and 1990 panels of *Richmond, 1737–2026* twenty-two towers are dated from published
  sources (Wikipedia's list of Richmond's tallest buildings, its James Center article) instead of
  the assessor's records, and three tall buildings with no source are left to 2026. The captions
  say what the early panels can show: before 1886, only the buildings that survive, the curated
  landmarks and the streets.
- Each print says that the ground is today's and that traced buildings' heights are assumed from
  their material, legibly on the smallest print sold.

What is approximate: the Free Bridge's line and its 8 m deck, the canal's course west of 6th
Street, the years the basin was cut back (about 1880) and gone (by 1895), and the dates of the
bridges where the history file says so. *Richmond, 1865* (RVA·02) stays off sale: with no
fire-insurance map before 1886 it shows only the buildings that survive and a few landmarks, which
reads as an empty city. The 1925 and 1950 prints (version 4) do not yet show the Free Bridge; they
will in their next version. The title block on each redrawn print gives version 3. This entry was
drafted with Claude.

## November 17, 2026

**Richmond, 1925 (plate RVA·01) and Richmond, 1950 (RVA·04), version 4: redrawn.** Both prints
were inspected again tile by tile after version 3 went on sale, and these faults in version 3 are
corrected:

- In 1925 the Mayo Bridge's south span, from Mayo's Island to Manchester, was missing. It is
  drawn whole.
- A tower finished in 1965, the Ross Building at 8th and Main Streets, stood in both years (the
  assessor dates it 1925). It is gone, and its lot is left empty.
- Traced Sanborn buildings stood in the river, on the canal, under the rail viaducts and in
  later embankments (the interstate's). They are left out.
- The Richmond and Petersburg railroad bridge ended in the air at the canal bank. It now lands on
  its approach to Byrd Street Station, and in 1925 the C&O runs to the Church Hill Tunnel (it
  collapsed that October); both approaches are placed approximately, from the USGS maps of 1934
  and 1956 and the 1924–25 Sanborn map.
- Modern roundabouts, the Canal Walk's canal bridges, parking-lot drives and planting beds were
  shown. They are left out.

The years that date later works (roundabouts from 1990, drives and parking from 1960, canal
bridges from 1999) are approximate, and every traced building's height is assumed from its
material, as the print says. The title block gives version 4.

## November 16, 2026

**The James, I-195 to Rocketts Landing (plate RVA·09), Richmond, 2026 (RVA·05), Richmond and
the James (RVA·08) and Gwynn's Island (GWY·01), version 4: redrawn.** The ground of each map is
painted in strips, from far to near. Where a wood, park or parking lot had a notch or a clearing
that crossed a strip's edge, the shape left a thin line of its own color across the open ground
inside it, one strip apart (about 170 faint lines in The James; in the two downtown views, on
Belle Isle and along the riverfront, among other places; on Gwynn's Island, in a few woods,
green spaces and a parking lot). Each shape is now cut to its strip as one outline, so the lines
are gone. Nothing else in the drawing changed; the title block on each print now gives version
4. On Gwynn's Island the credit line is also set larger, on two lines across the foot of the
print, so that it is legible at the smallest size the print is sold in (at least 7 pt on the
8 × 12 in print).

*Drafted with Claude; see the artist statement.*

**Richmond, 1925 (plate RVA·01), Richmond, 1950 (RVA·04), Richmond, 2026 (RVA·05) and Richmond
and the James (RVA·08), version 3: redrawn.** The four views of downtown Richmond were
inspected tile by tile, corrected in code, and drawn again from the same data.

- The same corrections as The James, below: the elevation is read in place, the hill shading
  is smooth, rapids and rocks are drawn only where the river falls, shorelines follow the
  mapped banks, and bridges and ramps end properly.
- The Capitol stands at its height: Jefferson's central block at 26 m (85 ft to the roof), with
  the wings of 1904–06 from 1906 (their 18 m is approximate). It had been a low 7 m block.
- St. John's Church, a one-story frame church, is drawn about 9 m tall (approximate) instead of
  at the 16 m guess for a church.
- Eleven buildings the city assessor misdated now stand only from their real years: the Hotel
  John Marshall (1929) is gone from 1925, and apartment buildings put up since 2005, which had
  appeared in the 1925 and 1950 views, are gone from both.
- In 1925 and 1950, buildings traced from Sanborn fire-insurance maps that would stand in
  today's river, or under the rail viaducts of 1901, are left out: the maps show buildings,
  not the old shorelines.
- In 1950, streets that now bridge roads built later (the expressway, I-95) run at grade, and
  surface parking lots (dated from about 1960) are left out.
- In 2026, two apartment buildings that OpenStreetMap tags far too tall are drawn by their
  floors: The Hues at City View (about 22 m, not 80 m) and The Locks at 321 (48 m, not 96 m).
  A building over the Haxall Canal stands on its banks instead of sinking below the ground in
  front of it.
- Each print's credit line names exactly the sources its map was drawn from, set large enough
  to read at the smallest size the print is sold in (at least 7 pt on an 8 × 12 in print). The
  1925 and 1950 prints add that the ground is today's, from lidar, so cuts and fills made
  since (the interstate, the floodwall) show in it.

The plates are unchanged; the title block on each print now gives version 3.

**The James, I-195 to Rocketts Landing (plate RVA·09) and Gwynn's Island (plate GWY·01),
version 3: redrawn.** Both maps were inspected tile by tile, corrected in code, and drawn again
from the same data.

- The elevation data had been read out of place, so the ground did not sit exactly
  under the mapped streets and shores. It is now read in place.
- The hill shading is smooth: steep banks no longer show dark smears or stepped edges.
- On the James, rapids and rocks are drawn only where the river falls, among the islands
  above the Mayo Bridge and at the dam by Belle Isle, and no longer on the dry bed south of
  Belle Isle or in a wastewater basin by Rocketts.
- Shorelines follow the mapped banks, without the saw-tooth edges they had along the tidal
  reach at Rocketts and on the bluffs and spit of Gwynn's Island.
- Bridges and ramps end properly, on the ground they reach, with no short steep stubs.
- On Gwynn's Island, the edges of woods and fields are smooth, no longer stepped by the
  30 m land-cover grid.
- Each print's credit line names exactly the sources its map was drawn from. Gwynn's Island
  had credited Sanborn fire-insurance maps it does not use; it now credits OpenStreetMap,
  USGS 3DEP elevation, the USGS National Land Cover Database and Microsoft's building
  footprints.

The plates are unchanged; the title block on each print now gives version 3.

*Drafted with Claude; see the [artist statement](artist-statement.md).*

**New: Broad Street Station, 1943: Cutaway (plate BSS·15, version 1).** The station cut down
the middle, from the Broad Street doors to the trains, with the same cut seen flat below it and a
traveler's way from the street to the train. It is the first building cutaway. Every part is
marked by how sure it is: drawn from a plan or section, from written accounts, or approximate.

## November 15, 2026

**Every print now carries its plate.** Each design has a permanent catalog number, its plate
(RVA·01 is Richmond, 1925; STC·02 is stop 2 of Richmond's Streetcars; LMA·25 is Alcatraz). A
small title block in the corner of every print and canvas gives the plate with the piece's place
in the catalog (collection, set and piece), its version, the year it was drawn, the point it was
drawn around and the direction it faces. On the two set sheets the block sits in the header. The
art is unchanged. Every design is now version 2, except the menu, the system map and stop 2 of
Richmond's Streetcars, 1925, which are version 3.

**New: Broad Street Station, 1943.** Richmond's union station at its wartime height and the
railroads that ran from it: the railroad map framed by fourteen places, the map alone, and a
print for each place. The first of a new kind of map, one building drawn close up in its own
time. [The places and their sources](broad-street.md)

**Richmond's Streetcars, 1925, version 2: the menu, the system map, and stop 2 (State Fair
Grounds).**

- The fair grounds were drawn as a rectangle square to north with a small oval in it, which
  matched neither the streets around them nor the sources. They are now traced from the 1925
  Sanborn fire-insurance sheet of the grounds: the triangle between the Boulevard and
  Hermitage Road, the fair's one-mile track (the note had said half-mile), and thirty-six of
  its buildings.
- Today's buildings, streets and lots on the site (The Diamond among them) are no longer
  drawn there in the years the fair stood.

The other thirteen stop prints are unchanged (version 1).
